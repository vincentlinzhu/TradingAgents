**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree that SMR is a high-volatility, pre-commercial nuclear option rather than a normal industrial stock. The bull case is legitimate: NuScale has scarce public-market exposure to small modular reactors, meaningful regulatory credibility, a very large cash balance, minimal long-term liabilities, and potential upside if AI/data-center power demand, government support, or utility partnerships translate into real projects. The improving short-term chart also gives traders a defined setup around the $13.65–$14.20 breakout zone and $12.00–$11.60 support. But the bear case carries more weight for portfolio positioning right now. SMR is still valued at a multi-billion-dollar market cap against only about $18.7 million of TTM revenue, revenue visibility has deteriorated sharply, Q1 missed expectations, analysts cut estimates, cash burn is heavy, dilution has already been severe, and Fluor’s equity exit weakens the strategic-alignment argument. The balance sheet gives SMR time, but time is not the same as commercial validation. Until NuScale proves binding customer demand, project financing, and cash-burn control, the risk/reward is too speculative to justify adding fresh exposure at current resistance. The right stance is cautious rather than outright bearish because the optionality is real, but the stronger arguments favor reducing exposure rather than chasing the story.

**Strategic Actions**: For SMR, trim exposure to a small speculative position only. Target a position size below normal risk allocation, roughly 0.5%–1.0% of portfolio NAV for aggressive portfolios and zero to minimal exposure for conservative mandates. Do not add into the current resistance area near $13.50–$13.65 unless there is a confirmed breakout above $13.65–$14.20 backed by real commercial news, not just thematic momentum. Use rallies into $14.20–$14.80 or $15.50–$16.20 to reduce if there is no accompanying contract, financing, or burn-rate catalyst. Maintain a hard risk discipline: a decisive break below $11.60 should trigger further trimming or exit of the speculative sleeve. Revisit the rating only if SMR announces binding customer agreements, credible project financing/government support, improving revenue visibility, and evidence that cash burn is moderating.
