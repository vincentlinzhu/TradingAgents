**Investment Thesis**: The risk debate supports selling SMR because the current setup combines stretched valuation, weak operating proof, heavy volatility, and repeated technical failure near resistance. The strongest bearish evidence is fundamental: SMR has only about $18.7 million of TTM revenue against a roughly $4.6 billion market capitalization, deeply negative net income around -$385.8 million, heavy free cash flow burn near -$200 million in recent quarters, a Q1 miss, deteriorating revenue visibility, and analyst estimate cuts. The company’s roughly $1.25 billion cash and short-term investment balance gives it time, but that liquidity came with severe shareholder dilution, with share count rising from about 122.8 million to 318.5 million, so the balance sheet is not a clean safety signal. Fluor’s equity exit further weakens the strategic-confidence argument, even though Fluor remains an EPC partner. Technically, SMR has improved from its April low near $8.85 and sits above short-term moving averages with positive momentum indicators, but the recovery has already delivered roughly a 50% rebound and has repeatedly stalled near $13.63–$13.67. With ATR around $1.13, or roughly 8.5% of the share price, downside to the $11.60 invalidation area is material, while near-term upside to $14.20–$14.80 is insufficient without hard news. The bull case remains real because SMR offers scarce public-market exposure to small modular reactors, regulatory credibility, nuclear-policy optionality, and potential upside from AI/data-center power demand; however, that optionality is not enough to justify meaningful capital at current resistance before binding customer agreements, credible project financing, improved revenue visibility, or moderating cash burn. Therefore, SMR should be sold now and kept on a watchlist for evidence-based re-entry rather than held as a core position.

**Time Horizon**: Immediate de-risking; reassess over 1-3 months or upon verified commercial/financing catalyst.
