And STM is structurally capital-intensive. That matters. Manufacturing assets can be a competitive advantage when demand is strong. But if demand disappoints, they become fixed-cost pressure, underutilized capacity, depreciation burden, and margin drag.

Bull says capex can become a moat. I say: **only if demand arrives on schedule.**

At this price, investors are being asked to assume that it will.

## 5. Inventory is a warning flag in the wrong context

Inventory rose to **$3.173 billion**, up from:

- **$3.136 billion** in Q4 2025
- **$3.014 billion** in Q1 2025

Bull says the increase is modest. I agree. But context matters.

Inventory is rising while:

- Revenue declined sequentially
- Gross margin declined sequentially
- Operating margin remains low
- SiC demand concerns are emerging
- Image-sensor competition is intensifying
- Auto and industrial markets remain cyclical

If demand improves, inventory can support sales. But if demand disappoints, inventory can turn into pricing pressure, margin compression, or write-down risk.

This is not a solvency issue. It is a margin-recovery issue — and margin recovery is exactly what the stock is already pricing in.

## 6. LEO is promising, but bulls are valuing it like proof

- More than **€3 billion through 2028**

Relative to TTM revenue of about **$12.4 billion**, that is meaningful.

We still need answers on:

- What are the margins?
- How concentrated are the customers?
- What is the timing of revenue recognition?
- How much R&D and capex is required?

Until LEO shows up in backlog, margins, and cash generation, it remains a promising narrative, not a proven earnings engine.

## 7. Edge AI sensors are optionality, not guaranteed upside

The new **VD55G4** and **VD65G4** ultralow-power image sensors are strategically interesting. But product launches do not equal material revenue, and revenue does not equal attractive margins.

The target markets — wearables, AR/VR, IoT, smart home, medical devices, edge devices — sound exciting. But many of these categories are fragmented, competitive, slow to scale, or price-sensitive.

AR/VR and wearables have disappointed investors before. Smart home and IoT are crowded. Medical can be attractive, but qualification cycles are long.

And competition is real. Sony remains a formidable image-sensor competitor, and onsemi-related commentary pointed to greater CMOS image-sensor competition.

Bull says STM is not trying to beat Sony in flagship smartphone cameras. Fine. But niche positioning still needs to become **material, profitable design wins**. Until then, this is optionality, not confirmed earnings.

## 8. Diversification does not eliminate cyclical risk

Bull argues that STM is diversified, so SiC softness does not invalidate the thesis. I agree it does not invalidate the whole company.

But it can absolutely hurt the stock.

The onsemi read-through flagged continued slowdown in silicon carbide. STM is exposed to power semiconductors, automotive electrification, industrial demand, and sensors — exactly the areas where weaker demand or pricing pressure could slow margin recovery.

Diversification reduces single-product risk. It does not remove cyclical risk.

And because STM’s valuation depends on operating leverage, even partial weakness in auto, industrial, SiC, or sensors can undermine the forward EPS case.

The balance sheet supports a **hold** for existing investors. It does not justify fresh buying after a near-parabolic rally.
