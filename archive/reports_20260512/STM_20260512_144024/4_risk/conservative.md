But redeployment should be selective, slower, and focused on opportunities with clearer downside support, stronger cash-flow visibility, and better margin of safety. The sale itself is the risk-management win.

On the upside case, I acknowledge that $62.25–$63.50 is not a hard ceiling. STM could overshoot if momentum buyers return or if the stock reclaims $60.20 on volume. But that does not make the current payoff attractive. From the high-$50s, that technical upside zone offers roughly mid-to-high single-digit potential. Meanwhile, downside to $53.00–$53.60 is close, downside to $50.00–$50.50 is plausible, and deeper mean reversion toward the 50-day average near $40.57 would be severe. That is not a risk/reward profile that justifies benchmark exposure.

The LEO and edge-AI narratives are credible, but they are still not de-risked. Reported space revenue expectations near €1 billion in 2026 and more than €3 billion through 2028 are encouraging, but we still need evidence of backlog, customer concentration, margins, contract durability, and free cash flow conversion. The ultralow-power image sensors strengthen the long-term story, but product launches are not the same as material earnings contribution. Competition from Sony in image sensors and the onsemi read-through on SiC softness remain real threats.

The analyst backdrop also supports caution. BofA and JPMorgan raised targets but stayed Neutral. UBS is more constructive, but the target cluster around the high-€40s suggests the Street is acknowledging the better story without aggressively endorsing further upside. That is not a signal to hold full exposure after a near-doubling. It is a signal that expectations have caught up quickly.

So my final view is unchanged. The Neutral approach is too slow and too reliant on the trend remaining intact. The Aggressive approach is right on the sale but too eager to make this about capital rotation. The safest path for the firm’s assets is disciplined de-risking.

Sell 25%–40% now. Reduce STM to below-benchmark. Keep only a small residual position while $55.60–$55.70 holds. If STM rebounds toward $62.25–$63.50 without hard evidence of margin expansion, positive free cash flow, stronger guidance, or confirmed LEO/sensor revenue conversion, use that strength to trim further. If STM closes decisively below $55.60–$55.70, especially on elevated volume, reduce again before $53 and $50 become the next downside targets.

STM may remain a strong long-term company, but the current setup is not conservative. The market has already paid aggressively for the recovery story. Our responsibility is to protect capital, reduce volatility, and require proof before rebuilding exposure. Final stance: SELL/trim 25%–40%.
