## 4. Valuation Analysis

### P/E Ratio: 34.86

The trailing P/E ratio of **34.86** is high. This suggests that investors are paying a premium for the earnings of the companies inside `QQQ`.

For a growth-heavy ETF, a premium multiple is normal, but this level leaves less room for disappointment. If earnings growth remains strong, especially among mega-cap technology and AI-related companies, the valuation can be sustained. However, if earnings revisions turn negative or rates rise, multiple compression could pressure the ETF.

**Trading implication:**  
`QQQ` is not obviously cheap. New long positions should be sized carefully, preferably on pullbacks rather than after sharp rallies.

---

### Price-to-Book Ratio: 2.00

The reported price-to-book ratio is approximately **2.00**. For an ETF like `QQQ`, this metric is less useful than for a bank, insurer, or industrial company because many underlying companies derive value from intangible assets such as software, intellectual property, network effects, and brand equity.

Still, the price-to-book ratio suggests that the ETF trades at a premium to the accounting book value of its holdings.

**Trading implication:**  
Price-to-book does not signal distress or deep value. `QQQ` should be viewed as a growth ETF, not a value ETF.

---

### Dividend Yield: 0.42%

The dividend yield of **0.42%** is very low. This is consistent with the ETF’s growth orientation. Many Nasdaq-100 companies either pay no dividend or return capital primarily through buybacks rather than income distributions.

**Trading implication:**  
`QQQ` is unsuitable for investors primarily seeking income. Returns depend overwhelmingly on price appreciation.
