Let’s start with the actual investment setup.

CRWV has:

- **Market cap:** about **$58.5B**
- **Enterprise value:** about **$76.8B**
- **TTM revenue:** about **$6.23B**
- **Price/sales:** roughly **9.4x**
- **EV/EBITDA:** roughly **25x**
- **TTM net income:** **-$1.59B**
- **Reported free cash flow:** **-$8.56B**
- **Total debt:** **$29.82B**
- **Net debt:** **$18.25B**
- **Current ratio:** **0.315**
- **Working capital:** about **-$8.95B**

That is a very aggressive financial profile. So when you say the stock has “reset” because it fell from **$137.98** to **$107.30**, I think that is dangerous anchoring. A stock can fall 22% and still be expensive. A stock can be well below its **$187** high and still have too much optimism embedded in the price.

The question is not whether CRWV is cheaper than it was. The question is whether the current valuation adequately compensates investors for the risks. I do not think it does.

## 1. “Capex is the strategy” is not enough

You keep saying CRWV’s negative free cash flow is acceptable because the company is building scarce AI infrastructure.

That may be true strategically, but it does not automatically make it good for shareholders.

CRWV spent roughly **$10.31B in capex during 2025** and generated about **-$7.25B of free cash flow**, with reported FCF around **-$8.56B**. That is not a rounding error. That is massive capital consumption.

The bull case assumes this spending will generate attractive returns. But that is precisely what remains unproven.

In infrastructure, growth can be value-destructive if the cost of capital rises, utilization disappoints, pricing compresses, or assets depreciate faster than expected. We have seen this movie before in telecom fiber, solar manufacturing, streaming infrastructure, and commodity cloud capacity. Demand can be huge, revenue can grow quickly, and equity holders can still lose if too much capital chases the same opportunity.

So yes, CRWV is building into demand. But shareholders need more than demand. They need proof that returns on that capital exceed the cost of debt, dilution, depreciation, power, construction, and competitive pressure.

That proof is not yet there.

---

## 2. Deferred revenue is visibility, not profitability

You point to **$8.19B of deferred revenue** as a major bull signal. I agree it is a positive demand indicator. It shows customers are committing to future capacity.

But deferred revenue does not equal free cash flow. It does not equal margin expansion. It does not equal equity value.

It means CRWV has obligations. The company still has to deliver the compute, secure power, complete data center buildouts, maintain service levels, avoid cost overruns, and do all of that profitably.

And CRWV is doing this with a weak liquidity profile:

- **Current assets:** about **$7.49B**
- **Current liabilities:** about **$16.44B**
- **Current debt and lease obligations:** **$7.17B**
- **Current ratio:** **0.315**

That is not financial flexibility. That is a company dependent on continued access to capital markets.

So yes, deferred revenue reduces demand uncertainty. But it does not eliminate execution risk. In fact, it raises the stakes: CRWV has promised capacity it must now fund, build, power, and deliver.

## 3. EBITDA is not enough in this business model

You keep emphasizing **$3.02B of TTM EBITDA** and **$741.3M of Q4 2025 EBITDA**. That is real scale. I am not dismissing it.

But for CRWV, EBITDA is a limited comfort because this is an asset-heavy, debt-funded infrastructure model. The business has enormous capex needs, heavy depreciation, and rising interest expense.

Q4 2025 interest expense was **$387.6M**, up from **$149.0M** in Q4 2024. That is more than a doubling year over year.

You argue that Q4 EBITDA covered interest by roughly **1.9x**, so it is not catastrophic. I see it differently. **1.9x EBITDA interest coverage is thin** for a company that is still burning billions in free cash flow and still needs external financing.

More importantly, Q4 operating income was **-$89.6M**. On an EBIT basis, CRWV did not cover interest expense at all.

That matters. EBITDA makes CRWV look healthier than the actual economics suggest. Equity holders ultimately need free cash flow, not adjusted comfort.
