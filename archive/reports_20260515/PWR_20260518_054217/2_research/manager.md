**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the important point: PWR is a high-quality, strategically relevant infrastructure leader with real exposure to AI power demand, grid modernization, electrification, utility capex, and energy reliability. The bull case is credible on business quality: revenue, net income, and free cash flow are growing strongly year over year; backlog visibility is strong; management has raised profit expectations; and the technical trend is still intact. But the bear case is stronger on the actual investment decision at the current price. PWR has already been aggressively re-rated, with the stock up roughly 77% year to date and 129% over one year, while trading around 47x forward earnings, over 100x TTM free cash flow, and a sub-1% FCF yield. For a low-margin, project-based business with working-capital volatility, rising debt, goodwill-heavy balance sheet risk, and meaningful execution risk, that leaves very little margin of safety. The bull is right that PWR deserves a premium, but the bear made the stronger argument that the current premium already discounts too much success. This is not a Sell because the long-term thesis is real and the trend is not broken, but it is not an attractive place to add capital. The right stance is cautious: respect the business, but reduce exposure to reflect poor near-term risk/reward.

**Strategic Actions**: For PWR, do not initiate new long exposure or chase a breakout near $785–789. If the portfolio is overweight PWR after the rally, trim exposure by roughly 25%–50% and bring the position below normal target weight while retaining only a smaller core for long-term AI/grid infrastructure exposure. Use $737, near VWMA support, as the key risk level: a decisive break below $737 should trigger another reduction. If PWR breaks into the $700–692 zone and fails to stabilize, cut further toward a minimal tracking position. Revisit adding only after either a meaningful valuation reset or a constructive base forms closer to $700, with improving cash conversion and margins. A deeper pullback toward $624–638 would be a more attractive area to reassess for accumulation if fundamentals remain intact.
