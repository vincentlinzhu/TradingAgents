Forward EPS of $2.23 and a forward P/E of 27.5x are only comforting if the recovery arrives on schedule. That is an assumption, not a margin of safety.

The actual reported recovery is mixed. Q1 revenue was up 23% year over year, but down 7.1% sequentially. Gross margin improved from the trough, but slipped from 35.2% in Q4 to 33.8% in Q1. Operating margin is only about 3.1%. Operating income was just $96 million on more than $3 billion of revenue. Q1 free cash flow was slightly negative. This is not yet a clean operating leverage story. It is an early recovery that the stock has already rewarded aggressively.

I also think the aggressive view is too forgiving on free cash flow. Heavy capex and acquisitions may support future growth, but they also increase execution risk. Capital investment only creates value if demand, utilization, pricing, and margins improve enough to generate attractive returns. In semiconductors, overinvestment into a choppy demand environment can pressure returns for years. STM’s strong balance sheet gives it resilience, but it does not make the equity low-risk at this price.

On BofA, the aggressive interpretation is too optimistic. A price target increase to EUR 49 from EUR 42 is constructive, but the maintained Neutral rating is important. The plain message is: improved outlook, but not enough conviction for a Buy. We should not treat a Neutral rating as a hidden bullish catalyst. From a risk standpoint, it confirms that the upside is more balanced after the rally.

On macro, I think both the aggressive and neutral views understate the risk of a sudden sentiment shift. Rising bond yields can pressure technology multiples. Rising oil prices revive inflation concerns. The failed Trump-Xi summit already hit semiconductor sentiment. STM is globally exposed and cyclical. Beta of 1.51 is not a feature for a conservative portfolio after a vertical rally; it is a warning that drawdowns can accelerate quickly.

On the onsemi SiC read-across, diversification helps, but it does not erase the risk. Automotive electrification, power semiconductors, and SiC are important parts of the STM bull case. If SiC demand is weaker than expected or pricing pressure rises, the forward EPS recovery becomes less certain. The new ultralow-power image sensors are strategically interesting, but product launches do not immediately fix weak operating margins, negative free cash flow, or sequential revenue pressure.

That is reasonable in theory, but after a 100%+ rally, the position’s risk profile has changed even if the portfolio weight is still technically within limits. Volatility has expanded. Valuation risk has increased. Downside gap risk has increased. The probability of a sharp pullback is materially higher than it was before.

A 15%–25% trim is not a bearish call. It is prudent gain protection. It allows the firm to retain core upside exposure while reducing the risk that a winning trade turns into a round trip. If STM breaks above 65.31 on strong volume, the remaining core still participates. But if it fails near resistance and rolls toward 58.70 or 56.5, the firm has already locked in part of the gain.

So my final stance is still HOLD, but it should be a guarded, risk-reduced HOLD, not an aggressive hold and not merely an “actively managed” hold with trimming as an afterthought.

The safest path for the firm is to protect gains, reduce exposure where appropriate, avoid new buying, and require consolidation or stronger proof of margin and free cash flow recovery before becoming more constructive.
