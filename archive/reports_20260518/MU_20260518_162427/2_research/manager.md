**Rationale**: Both sides made credible points on MU. The bull case is strongest on current fundamentals: revenue growth near 196% YoY, exceptional margins, strong free cash flow, net cash, falling inventory despite surging sales, and clear AI/HBM demand tailwinds. MU is not a weak company, and the forward P/E looks optically very low if the forward EPS estimate holds. However, the bear side carried the debate on risk/reward at the current entry point. MU has already had a parabolic move, sentiment is crowded around the AI-memory thesis, margins look peak-like for a historically cyclical memory manufacturer, and the forward valuation depends heavily on earnings durability that is not yet proven. The May 18 reversal on heavy volume adds tactical risk, especially with MU still far above major moving averages and vulnerable to a 20%+ reset without breaking the long-term trend. The bull is right that MU is not broken, but the bear is more persuasive that buying aggressively here offers insufficient margin of safety after such a large move. The right stance is not a full Sell because the operating momentum is genuinely strong, but we should reduce exposure and wait for either a cleaner technical base or more confirmation that AI memory earnings are durable.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim MU exposure into rebounds rather than add at current levels. For existing positions, reduce to a below-benchmark/underweight allocation, especially if MU rallies into the $725–$757 resistance zone and fails to hold momentum. Do not initiate new aggressive long exposure here. Keep a small residual position only for accounts that want AI-memory exposure, but size it modestly given MU’s high beta and expanded ATR.
