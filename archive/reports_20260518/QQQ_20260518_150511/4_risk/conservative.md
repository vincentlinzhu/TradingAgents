That is exactly the kind of risk a conservative desk must take seriously.

To the neutral analyst, I agree with most of the conditional Hold framework, but I would lean more defensive in implementation. If QQQ is already at target weight in a diversified portfolio, I agree we do not need to cut below target without confirmation. But if QQQ has become overweight after this rally — or if the portfolio has overlapping exposure through Nvidia, semiconductors, AI infrastructure, or mega-cap tech — trimming should happen now, not after the break. Rebalancing excess exposure is not market timing. It is basic risk control.

I also think hedging should be treated as more than an optional afterthought. I am not arguing for blanket hedging of every target-weight position. But where total AI/tech exposure is high, a short-dated put spread, collar, or partial rotation into broader market or defensive exposure is prudent. The cost of hedging must be weighed against the cost of a catalyst-driven gap through support. In this setup, some risk transfer is reasonable.

So my final conservative stance is clear: Hold, but make it a defensive Hold.

Maintain core target-weight QQQ exposure because the intermediate and long-term trend remains intact.
