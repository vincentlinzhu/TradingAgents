**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides made credible points on MU. The bull case is strongest on the fundamentals: revenue, margins, EPS, free cash flow, inventory control, and balance-sheet strength all point to a real AI-driven memory upcycle rather than just narrative hype. MU also has direct exposure to higher-value server DRAM/HBM-style demand, tight supply, and improving product mix, and the forward valuation still looks inexpensive if earnings durability is even partly real. The bear case is strongest on timing and risk/reward: MU has rallied more than 600% in a year, margins look potentially peak-like, price-to-book is elevated for a capital-intensive memory company, the chart is stretched and volatile, and the $650–$652 support zone is critical. The bear also correctly warns that MU can fall sharply even if the long-term thesis remains intact, especially with crowded AI-semiconductor positioning, rising yields, and event risk around AI infrastructure sentiment. My read is that the bull arguments win on medium-term fundamentals, but the bear arguments are strong enough to avoid an outright Buy at this entry. MU should be owned, but not chased aggressively.

**Strategic Actions**: Maintain or build an Overweight position in MU, but implement it gradually. For existing holders, keep a core long position while MU remains above the $650–$684 support zone; trim only if the position has become oversized from the rally. For new capital, scale in rather than buying all at once: initiate a partial position now or on pullbacks toward $684–$650, then add only if support holds. Add more aggressively only on a confirmed reclaim of $725–$747 with strong volume, as that would signal momentum is turning back up. Use a decisive close below $650–$652 as the risk-management trigger to pause additions, reduce exposure, or hedge, since that could open downside toward $600, $576–$607, and potentially the 50-day area near $492. Position size should be below a full high-conviction Buy allocation given MU’s elevated ATR, high beta, and cyclical risk; target a modest overweight rather than an outsized position.
