**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree STM is a high-quality semiconductor company with a strong balance sheet, relevant exposure to automotive, industrial, power semis, microcontrollers, sensors, SiC, and low-power edge devices, and a recovery setup after trough profitability. The bull case is strongest on STM’s resilience and operating leverage: Q1 revenue was up about 23% year over year, gross margin is well above the 2025 trough, operating cash flow was positive, and the stock remains in a confirmed uptrend. But the bear case carries the debate at the current price. STM has already rallied sharply to around $61, near the $65.31 high, while current profitability remains very weak, free cash flow is still negative, revenue declined sequentially, gross margin slipped from Q4, and the forward valuation depends heavily on a large earnings rebound materializing. The technical picture is bullish but stretched, with STM far above its 50-day and 200-day averages and momentum cooling. So this is not a broken-company call; it is a risk/reward call. STM is worth keeping on the book, but new capital should not chase here, and strength should be used to manage exposure.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim STM modestly into strength rather than add at current levels. For existing positions, reduce exposure by roughly 20%–30% if STM trades back into the $63–$65 area or fails again near the $65.31 high. Do not initiate new full-sized positions here. Maintain only a smaller core position to preserve upside participation if the recovery thesis continues to develop. Reconsider adding only on either a pullback toward $55–$58 with stabilization, ideally closer to $52.50–$53.00 for better risk/reward, or after clear evidence of margin expansion and positive free cash flow. Use $57.90–$58.00 as the key tactical risk level; a high-volume break below that zone should trigger further trimming. If STM breaks above $65.31 on strong volume, avoid chasing aggressively; wait for confirmation that the breakout holds before reassessing position size.
