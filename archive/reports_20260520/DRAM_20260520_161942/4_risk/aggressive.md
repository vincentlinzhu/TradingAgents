Now to the neutral analyst: the repeated argument is that “DRAM is extended and crowded, but not broken.” That is technically true, but it is also the wrong standard. Waiting until a momentum trade is clearly broken is how traders give back the best part of a parabolic gain.

DRAM is barely above its short-term supports. The 10 EMA is around $49.74, VWMA is around $50.10, and price is $51.51. That cushion is tiny compared with ATR of $3.26, or about 6.3% of price. But by then, the same signal will be obvious to everyone. The aggressive edge is not selling after confirmation; it is reducing exposure before confirmation becomes consensus.

The neutral analyst also leans on RSI around 62 and positive MACD as evidence that the trend is still constructive. But direction matters more than the absolute level after a parabolic run. RSI has fallen from above 85 to roughly 62. MACD rolled down from 4.15 to 3.47. That is loss of acceleration. In a normal, steady uptrend, that can be healthy. In a newly launched thematic ETF that has reportedly pulled in around $9 billion in assets in six weeks, including a $903 million daily inflow and more than $250 million in retail inflows, fading acceleration is a real warning. Momentum vehicles need fresh acceleration. When acceleration cools, late buyers become unstable holders.

The neutral analyst says upside could overshoot above $58–$59 if Nvidia validates HBM scarcity. Sure. But that requires a lot to go right. Nvidia needs to be more than good; it needs to be spectacular relative to expectations. AI capex commentary needs to stay hot. Memory bottlenecks need to remain severe. ETF inflows need to keep coming. Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron need to avoid any execution or guidance wobble. That is a perfection stack.

The downside does not need perfection. It only needs “good but not good enough.”

That is why Nvidia earnings are not a reason to hold full exposure. They are a reason to reduce now. DRAM has become a high-beta second-derivative AI trade. If Nvidia beats but guides merely in line, if management hints at easing supply constraints, if margins show component pressure, or if the market simply sells the news after a massive AI run-up, DRAM is exactly the kind of crowded thematic vehicle that gets hit first.

But the 40.97 trailing P/E still matters because the ETF has already rallied 85%–98% on the expectation that forward earnings will improve. The market has not ignored the HBM thesis; it has aggressively capitalized it. At this point, “forward earnings could catch up” is not a margin of safety. It is the consensus bull case.

Correct — and that is exactly why position size should come down after a vertical move. Concentration is a weapon when the theme is under-owned. It becomes a trapdoor when the theme is crowded, expensive, and momentum is cooling. One weak memory-pricing comment, one earnings disappointment, one Korea/Asia semiconductor risk-off event, or one ETF-flow reversal can pressure the whole vehicle at once.
