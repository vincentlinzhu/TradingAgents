Even then, the add should be smaller than a pullback add because the stock would still be extended.

On sizing, I would not endorse the aggressive path to 1.5x normal weight yet. I also would not restrict the position so tightly that we miss the trend. A reasonable compromise is to maintain existing exposure and allow staged adds up to roughly 1.1x–1.25x normal weight under clean confirmation. Going beyond that should require fundamental evidence: gross margin sustainably improving above the mid-30% range, operating margin moving meaningfully above low-single digits, and free cash flow turning consistently positive.

On risk control, I agree with a layered stop framework. The $55.5 hard stop can remain as the deeper structural stop for the residual core position, especially given ATR around $2.88. But it should not be the only line of defense. A decisive close below $60–61 should trigger review and likely partial reduction if accompanied by heavy volume, weak semiconductor breadth, rising yields, or negative U.S.-China/trade headlines. However, a brief undercut followed by a quick reclaim should not force an automatic exit.

So my final challenge to both sides is this: Aggressive is right to respect the trend, but too willing to press before fundamentals confirm. Conservative is right to respect valuation and free-cash-flow risk, but too willing to let fear of drawdown reduce participation in a still-valid uptrend.

The best risk-adjusted path is neither offensive overweight nor defensive retreat. It is disciplined participation: hold the core, do not chase at $65–66, add only on a clean $60–61 reset or a genuinely high-quality $66 breakout, cap exposure around 1.25x normal for now, and use $60–61 as the tactical warning zone with $55.5 as the deeper hard stop. That keeps upside exposure alive while preventing a strong chart from turning into oversized risk.
