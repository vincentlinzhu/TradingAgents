STM is not a direct AI accelerator leader. It is an indirect beneficiary through analog, power, industrial, and infrastructure demand. If investors begin separating direct AI earnings beneficiaries from second-order beneficiaries, STM could see multiple compression even if the company remains fundamentally sound.

So I would not soften the trader’s plan into a mild trim/HOLD. The proposed 30%–50% reduction is appropriate, and I would favor the higher end if STM has become a meaningful contributor to portfolio volatility or concentration risk. A 25% trim may look measured, but it may leave too much exposure to a very normal 10%–20% reset after a parabolic move.

The low-risk path is straightforward: sell into the $70–$71 strength, reduce STM to below-benchmark tactical exposure, and keep only controlled residual participation in case the stock breaks above $71.07. Do not initiate a new full position at this level. If STM closes below $66.50–$66.86, especially on heavy volume, reduce further. Reassess only after a healthier pullback toward $63–$64 or after clear evidence of durable margin improvement, positive free cash flow, and credible EPS recovery.

STM may continue higher, but the conservative question is not “can it go higher?” The question is whether the firm should risk a large portion of an extraordinary gain for uncertain incremental upside when valuation, volatility, sentiment, and macro risks have all become less favorable. My answer remains no.
