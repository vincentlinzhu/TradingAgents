## 4. The bull says flows equal demand. I say flows can become forced selling.

The bull’s strongest claim is that ETF flows validate the thesis. DRAM being in the top 10 for daily ETF flows, with more than **$1 billion added in one day**, sounds bullish.

But flows are not fundamentals. Flows are investor behavior. And investor behavior can reverse quickly.

In a thematic ETF, flows create a feedback loop:

1. Price rises.
2. Media coverage increases.
3. More investors buy the ETF.
4. ETF buys underlying holdings.
5. Underlying holdings rise.
6. ETF performance improves.
7. More investors chase.

That works beautifully on the way up. But the same mechanism works in reverse.

If semiconductor sentiment weakens, if Korean memory stocks gap lower, if NVIDIA or hyperscalers say anything that reduces AI capex enthusiasm, or if memory pricing expectations peak, ETF outflows could pressure the underlying holdings and amplify downside.

The bull calls $1 billion of daily inflows “demand.” I call it **potential late-cycle FOMO**. Especially when the social/news report describes sentiment as **very bullish / euphoric** and related leveraged memory products have posted extreme gains.

Crowded trades do not need bad news to correct. Sometimes they correct when good news stops being better than expected.

The bull argument depends on a very favorable forward scenario:

- Memory pricing power persists.
- Supply does not catch up too quickly.
- Earnings revisions outpace price appreciation.

If any of those assumptions weaken, a 50x trailing multiple leaves little margin of safety.
