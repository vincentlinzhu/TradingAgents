The bull says current revenue is the wrong anchor. Fine — but then what is the anchor? Hope? Scarcity? Strategic relevance? Those can support a narrative, but they do not protect investors if expectations reset.

That is a very narrow path.

## 2. The revenue ramp is real, but the quality of the ramp is less impressive than the bull suggests.

- Q4 2025 revenue: **$61.9 million**
- Q1 2026 revenue: **$64.7 million**

That is only modest sequential growth. The huge year-over-year comparison benefits from an extremely small starting base.

More importantly, the cost structure is moving in the wrong direction.

In Q1 2026:

- Revenue: **$64.7 million**
- Gross profit: **$15.4 million**
- Gross margin: about **23.8%**
- Operating expenses: **$286.9 million**
- Operating income: **-$271.5 million**

That means IONQ generated **$15.4 million of gross profit** while spending nearly **$287 million** on operating expenses. This is not a company showing operating leverage yet. It is a company whose losses are scaling faster than its proven profitability.

And gross margin matters. TTM gross margin was around **36%**, but Q1 gross margin was closer to **24%**. For a company valued on future scalability, margin compression is not a small detail. If the business model requires heavy customization, hardware costs, integration costs, or acquisition-related expenses, then the path to attractive economics may be harder than bulls assume.
