## 7. Capex is a double-edged sword

You call MU’s capex a moat. Sometimes it is. But in memory, capex is also how cycles turn.

MU’s recent capex is heavy:

- Latest quarter: **$6.39B**.
- Prior quarter: **$5.39B**.
- Two quarters ago: **$5.66B**.

Yes, MU still generated **$5.52B** of free cash flow after capex. That’s impressive. But the broader industry has the same incentive to invest into elevated pricing.

Capex is a moat only if demand stays ahead of supply. If the industry overbuilds into peak pricing, capex becomes a trap.

The bull assumption is that AI demand absorbs everything. Maybe for now. But at a trillion-dollar valuation, “for now” may not be enough.

## 8. Free cash flow is strong, but the valuation is not cheap on current cash generation

MU’s latest free cash flow of **$5.52B** is strong. But against a market cap around **$1.095T**, annualizing that quarterly FCF gives roughly **$22B** of annualized FCF.

That is only about a **2% free-cash-flow yield**.

So investors are not buying cheap current cash flow. They are paying for a major future expansion in free cash flow.

That expansion may happen. But it leaves little margin of safety if:

- Margins normalize.
- Capex stays elevated.
- Pricing weakens.
- Customers digest inventory.
- Competitors catch up.
- AI capex expectations cool.
- Macro conditions pressure high-beta tech.

The stronger balance sheet helps reduce financial risk. It does not eliminate valuation risk.

## 9. Receivables are not a crisis, but they deserve respect

You’re right that receivables should rise when revenue rises. But the magnitude still matters.

Accounts receivable rose from **$8.01B** to **$15.39B** quarter over quarter. Revenue rose from **$13.64B** to **$23.86B**.

Receivables as a percentage of quarterly revenue increased from roughly **59%** to about **65%**. That is not a disaster, but it is something investors should watch closely.

In cyclical industries, stress often appears first in working capital:

- Receivables.
- Inventories.
- Payment terms.
- Pull-forward demand.
- Double-ordering.
- Customer digestion.

Operating cash flow is strong today. But at this valuation, MU does not have much room for subtle warning signs to become real problems.

## 10. Sentiment is crowded, and crowded trades are fragile

After a 79% monthly rally, the bar is very high.

## 11. Macro risk matters more for a beta-1.9 stock

MU has a reported beta of **1.919**. This is not a defensive compounder. It is a high-beta semiconductor leader in a concentrated AI-driven market.

The macro backdrop includes:

- Elevated high-beta risk appetite.

If yields rise, oil spikes, inflation surprises, or investors rotate away from AI hardware, MU is exactly the kind of stock that gets sold first. Not because the company is bad, but because investors have large gains to protect.

In risk-off markets, correlations rise. MU will not be immune.

## 12. “Staged buying” does not solve the problem
