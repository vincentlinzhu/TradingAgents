FINAL TRANSACTION PROPOSAL: **HOLD**

# Weekly Trading & Macro Report — `NOW`  
**Date:** 2026-05-29  
**Instrument:** ServiceNow, `NOW`

## Executive View

`NOW` had a major sentiment-driven rebound this week, reportedly rising roughly **12.7%–14% on May 29** as investors rotated back into enterprise software and AI workflow names. The move was not driven by fresh `NOW` earnings or a company-specific product announcement; rather, it was powered by a broader reassessment of software after strong AI-related results from **Snowflake** and **Dell**.

The key market debate has shifted from “AI will destroy SaaS” toward “AI will increase demand for platforms embedded in enterprise workflows.” That is supportive for `NOW`, which sits at the intersection of enterprise automation, IT service management, workflow orchestration, and AI-enabled productivity.

However, after a sharp one-day rally, the risk/reward is less attractive for fresh entry. The rally appears partly sympathy-driven and momentum-based. For traders already long, the setup supports **holding** exposure, but chasing the move aggressively after a 12%–14% jump is less compelling.

---

## 1. `NOW` Company & Sector News

### `NOW` rallied sharply as software sentiment improved

Multiple reports indicated `NOW` was one of the major beneficiaries of a renewed AI/software rally:

- `NOW` shares reportedly rose **12.7%** after a “two-day wave of AI conviction” linked to Snowflake and Dell.
- Another report said `NOW` rose around **14%**, trading near **$124** after closing the prior day near **$108.73**.
- The rally was described as part of a broader rotation into beaten-down enterprise software names.

The important point: this was a **sector re-rating**, not a direct `NOW` earnings catalyst. That makes the move powerful but potentially fragile if broader AI sentiment fades.

### “SaaSpocalypse” fears are easing

A recurring theme across the week’s software coverage was that fears of a software collapse caused by AI may have been overstated. Reports described a prior “SaaSpocalypse” narrative, where investors feared AI agents could replace traditional subscription software and pressure software valuations.

Snowflake’s strong results helped challenge that view. Investors appeared to conclude that large software platforms may benefit from AI if they control enterprise data, workflows, identity, security, and automation layers.

That is relevant for `NOW` because ServiceNow’s platform is deeply embedded in enterprise processes, especially IT, HR, security, and customer workflows. In an AI-agent world, enterprises may need more structured workflow orchestration, governance, ticketing, auditability, and integration — all areas where `NOW` has strategic relevance.

### Relative valuation narrative is improving

One article contrasted Palantir trading at roughly **42x forward sales** with ServiceNow at around **6x forward sales**, arguing that `NOW` may be comparatively attractive among AI-related software names.

This kind of relative valuation argument can support institutional rotation into `NOW`, especially if investors want AI exposure but are wary of extreme multiples in more speculative names.

That said, the article also noted that investors must look beyond headline valuation. `NOW` is not a cheap cyclical stock; it remains a high-quality growth software franchise, and its valuation depends on sustained enterprise spending, margin execution, and successful AI monetization.

## 2. Macro & Market Environment

### U.S. equities are risk-on again

Global market news showed a strong risk-on tone:

- U.S. stock indexes rallied to record highs.
- Market gains were attributed to AI spending optimism and hopes around geopolitical de-escalation.
- Dell’s blowout AI-server results helped reinforce the AI capital expenditure cycle.

This environment is supportive for growth equities and high-duration software names like `NOW`. When indexes are making highs and investors are rewarding AI-related growth, `NOW` can benefit from multiple expansion.

### AI infrastructure strength is spilling over into software

Dell’s AI-server demand was a major catalyst. Its strong results suggested that AI spending remains robust at the infrastructure layer. The key question for `NOW` is whether infrastructure spending eventually translates into software monetization.

This week, the market answered “yes” — or at least became more willing to price in that possibility.

The trading implication is that `NOW` may continue to move with the broader AI basket, even though its business is not infrastructure hardware. Traders should monitor:

- Dell and AI-server demand signals.
- Snowflake and data-platform results.
- Enterprise software earnings from names like Salesforce, Workday, Oracle, Okta, Zscaler, and Datadog.
- Commentary on AI agents, workflow automation, and enterprise AI adoption.

### Inflation and energy remain the main macro risks

Global news also highlighted stubborn inflation and a sharp gasoline price move, with one report noting gas rising from **$2.98 to $4.39**. Higher fuel prices can pressure consumers, raise inflation expectations, and complicate central bank policy.

For `NOW`, the inflation/oil risk is indirect but important:

- Higher inflation can keep rates elevated.
- Higher rates pressure long-duration growth valuations.
- A recession scare could delay enterprise software spending.
- CIOs may scrutinize large SaaS contracts more aggressively if macro conditions deteriorate.

At the moment, equity markets are looking through these risks, but they remain important downside catalysts.

### Geopolitical risk has eased but not disappeared

Markets also rallied on hopes tied to reduced Middle East tensions and reports of a U.S.–Iran ceasefire extension or maritime restrictions easing around the Strait of Hormuz.

That matters because energy markets are highly sensitive to geopolitical disruption. If oil or gasoline prices spike again, the risk-on AI/software rally could weaken quickly.

## 3. Trading Implications for `NOW`

### Bull case

The bullish setup for `NOW` is stronger than it was during the peak “AI destroys SaaS” narrative.

Key positives:

1. **Enterprise AI rotation:** Investors are again willing to buy software names that could benefit from AI adoption.
2. **Workflow relevance:** `NOW` is positioned around enterprise automation and workflow management, which may become more important as companies deploy AI agents.
3. **Relative valuation:** Compared with more speculative AI names, `NOW` appears less expensive on forward sales metrics.
4. **Market breadth:** The AI trade is broadening beyond chips and hardware into infrastructure, data, security, and software.
5. **Quality franchise:** ServiceNow remains a leading enterprise platform with sticky customers and mission-critical use cases.

### Bear case

Despite the rally, there are reasons to avoid chasing:

1. **Move was not company-specific:** The rally was mostly sympathy-driven from Dell and Snowflake.
2. **Short-term overextension:** A 12%–14% daily move can invite profit-taking.
3. **AI monetization still needs proof:** Investors are assuming enterprise AI will support software revenue growth; that still needs confirmation through earnings.
4. **Macro sensitivity:** Inflation, oil, and recession risk can hit software multiples.
5. **Crowded AI trade:** If AI momentum unwinds, high-beta beneficiaries like `NOW` could reverse sharply.

---

## 4. Recommended Positioning

### Base case: **HOLD**

For existing holders, the recommendation is to **hold** `NOW`, not sell into the first major software recovery unless risk limits require trimming. The rally suggests the market may be revaluing ServiceNow as a durable AI-era workflow platform rather than as a vulnerable SaaS incumbent.

However, for new buyers, the better risk/reward is likely to wait for either:

- A pullback after the sharp rally,
- A consolidation above the breakout level,
- Or company-specific confirmation from management, earnings, billings, RPO, or AI product adoption metrics.

### Tactical trading view

- **Existing long:** Hold core position; consider trimming only if position size became too large after the rally.
- **New entry:** Avoid chasing immediately after a 12%–14% jump; look for pullbacks or consolidation.
- **Aggressive trader:** Could buy dips if software momentum remains broad and indexes hold highs.
- **Risk control:** If the AI/software rotation fades quickly or macro risk spikes through oil/inflation, reduce exposure.

### Key indicators to watch

For `NOW`, traders should monitor:

1. **Software ETF and SaaS peer performance**
   - If the broader software rally continues, `NOW` likely remains supported.
2. **AI infrastructure earnings**
   - Dell, Nvidia, Supermicro, Broadcom, and cloud capex commentary remain important read-throughs.
3. **Enterprise software earnings**
   - Snowflake, Oracle, Salesforce, Workday, Okta, Zscaler, and Datadog commentary can influence `NOW`.
4. **Oil and gasoline prices**
   - A renewed energy spike could pressure risk assets.
5. **Interest rates**
   - Higher yields would pressure growth multiples.
6. **ServiceNow AI monetization**
   - Watch for adoption of GenAI products, pricing power, renewal trends, and large-deal activity.

---

## 5. Bottom Line

`NOW` is benefiting from a powerful shift in market psychology. The market is no longer treating AI solely as a threat to SaaS; it is increasingly viewing certain software platforms as AI beneficiaries. ServiceNow fits that narrative because it controls enterprise workflows and automation layers that could become more valuable as AI adoption scales.

Still, the stock’s latest move appears driven by broader AI/software momentum rather than new `NOW`-specific fundamentals. That makes the correct stance **constructively neutral**: hold existing exposure, but wait for a better entry before adding aggressively.

**Final view on `NOW`: HOLD.**
