However, the conservative and neutral analysts made the stronger risk-management argument against approving the trader’s proposal as a full Buy at current levels. MU is technically stretched: about 23% above its latest 10 EMA, 86% above its 50 SMA, more than 200% above its 200 SMA, above the Bollinger framework, with RSI near 78 and ATR around $56. The stock has also moved roughly 38% since May 22, about 183% since early April, and more than 260% year to date per the debate, creating real risk that even a normal volatility reset could pressure late entries. The $971 stop is useful, but it is not true downside insurance, especially if entries occur near $1,035 or above $1,046.97, where the actual distance to the stop is closer to 6%–7% before slippage and gap risk.

The final decision therefore balances participation with restraint. MU should not be downgraded to Hold because the fundamental momentum, AI/HBM scarcity thesis, analyst revision support, cash generation, and improving balance sheet justify above-benchmark exposure. But MU also should not be treated as a clean Buy because sentiment is crowded, the chart is parabolic, receivables of $17.314 billion require monitoring, capex creates future supply-risk questions, and June 24 earnings carry a high bar where merely good guidance could trigger a sell-the-news reaction. The right portfolio action is disciplined Overweight: own MU, add gradually on support or confirmed strength, keep pre-earnings sizing controlled, and only consider 125%–150% of normal exposure after a healthier consolidation or earnings confirmation of HBM demand, DRAM/NAND pricing strength, margin durability, cash conversion, and upward estimate revisions.
