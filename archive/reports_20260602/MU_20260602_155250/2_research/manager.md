**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull side made a strong case that MU’s business momentum is real: AI/HBM demand, explosive revenue and EPS growth, strong free cash flow, falling debt, and a forward valuation that does not look demanding if the current earnings trajectory holds. That argues against treating MU as a broken story or an outright sell. But the bear side carried the debate on current risk/reward. MU has moved nearly vertically, sentiment is crowded, volatility has expanded, and the stock is extremely stretched versus key moving averages. More importantly, the valuation defense depends heavily on forward EPS and unusually high margins being durable in a historically cyclical memory business. Even if the AI-memory thesis remains intact, a normal technical reset could be 15–25%, and if DRAM/HBM expectations soften, earnings and multiple compression could hit together. So the right call is not to abandon MU as a long-term AI-memory winner, but to reduce exposure at this price and wait for a healthier entry.

**Strategic Actions**: Move MU below target weight rather than add here. For existing positions, trim oversized exposure by roughly 25–50%, especially if MU has grown materially above intended portfolio weight after the rally. Do not initiate fresh full-size longs at current levels. Keep only a smaller core position if the trader wants continued AI-memory exposure, and protect it with trailing stops or hedges. Treat a close below $1,000 as a warning signal; watch $970–$980, $928–$950, and the 10-day EMA area near $876 as potential reassessment zones. Rebuild exposure only after MU consolidates, volatility cools, or pulls back meaningfully while DRAM/HBM pricing, AI capex, margins, inventory, receivables, and free cash flow remain supportive.
