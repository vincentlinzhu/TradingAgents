Overbought momentum below long-term resistance is different from overbought momentum in an established uptrend. A pullback toward $118–$120, or even $110–$112, would not break the long-term thesis; it would improve the risk/reward.

I also do not accept the aggressive claim that sector-driven weakness is automatically a buying opportunity. For high-multiple software stocks, sector-driven weakness is often the primary risk. ETF flows, AI rotation, rates, valuation compression, and retail crowding can overwhelm company-specific fundamentals. The reports specifically cite software-sector whipsaw, profit-taking, and record retail inflows into software ETFs. That may become upside fuel, but it can just as easily become unstable exit pressure. We already saw NOW sell off sharply while the broader market improved. That is exactly the kind of factor-risk behavior we should respect.

On valuation, the aggressive analyst is too comfortable assuming the forward case plays out. Forward P/E around 25x, PEG near 1.21, and roughly 3.9% FCF yield are defensible, but they are not a margin-of-safety valuation. The stock still trades around 76x trailing earnings and more than 11x book. Q1 GAAP net income grew only about 2% year over year, operating income growth lagged revenue growth, stock-based compensation was 14.5% of revenue, and buybacks exceeded quarterly free cash flow. Those are not reasons to sell a high-quality company, but they are reasons not to chase an extended technical setup.

The acquisition risk also should not be brushed aside. Goodwill and intangibles rose to $6.02 billion, and Q1 business purchases were $1.325 billion. Maybe those acquisitions deepen the AI moat. But maybe they create integration risk, amortization pressure, or future impairment risk. A conservative process should not underwrite acquisitions as value-accretive before there is evidence.

Requiring a closing hold above $124–$125, reducing the first tranche, and treating $99 as only a disaster stop are all improvements. But I still think even the neutral plan risks being too eager unless the conditions are very strict. A 15%–20% starter may be acceptable only if there is a clear closing defense of $124–$125, selling volume cools, and software-sector breadth stabilizes. I would not support buying simply because the stock touches $124.50 intraday.

My preferred recommendation remains HOLD. We should wait for either a controlled pullback into $118–$120 with visible stabilization and a cooled RSI, or a decisive reclaim of $141–$142 with follow-through. The first gives us better entry discipline. The second confirms that the long-term trend is repairing. Both are safer than buying the first support test after a vertical rally.

If the desk insists on participating now, I would materially reduce the risk: cap the initial tranche at 10%–15% of intended incremental exposure, require a closing hold above $124–$125 with cooling selling pressure, use $124–$125 as an immediate review level, treat $118–$120 as a decision zone rather than an automatic add, and treat $110–$112 as the practical tactical failure area. The $99 stop should only be a last-resort long-term invalidation point, not the main risk control.

ServiceNow is a strong company, but this is not a low-risk entry. The stock is extended, overbought, volatile, below long-term resistance, exposed to crowded AI/software flows, and priced for continued execution. The aggressive plan may work if momentum resumes immediately, but it exposes the firm to avoidable drawdown if the breakout fails. For protecting assets, the safer path is patience, not a full BUY as written.
