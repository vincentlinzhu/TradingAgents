**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: The bull side carried the fundamental argument on MU: AI-driven HBM and advanced DRAM demand appears real, supply is tight, pricing power is showing up in the numbers, and the latest quarter showed a genuine earnings inflection with explosive revenue growth, very high margins, strong free cash flow, stable inventory, and a net cash balance sheet. That makes it hard to treat MU as just an old-cycle commodity memory name. The forward valuation also looks reasonable if estimates are directionally right. The bear side made the better tactical argument: MU is extremely extended after a near-vertical move, sentiment is crowded, the June 24 earnings bar is high, margins may be peak-like, and the forward P/E relies on aggressive EPS assumptions. Those are real risks, especially for new money chasing the highs. But the bear case mostly argues for disciplined sizing and entry timing rather than abandoning the stock. Net-net, MU deserves exposure, but not a full-force chase at the current technical extension.

**Strategic Actions**: Move MU to a modest Overweight, but build exposure in stages rather than chasing aggressively. For existing holders, maintain a core position and only trim if MU has become oversized; use trailing stops or short-dated hedges into the June 24 earnings event if position risk is large. For new or underweight accounts, initiate only a partial position now, around one-third to one-half of intended size, and reserve capital to add on pullbacks toward roughly $1,020, $1,000, and especially the $964–$971 support area. Do not use leverage given MU’s beta and expanded ATR. If MU breaks and holds above $1,100 on strong volume, allow the position to work and consider a small momentum add, but avoid forcing size after a vertical move. If MU loses $1,038 and then $1,000 on heavy volume before earnings, pause additional buying and reassess momentum risk. After earnings, add more only if management confirms durable HBM demand, strong pricing, healthy free cash flow conversion, disciplined capex, and no deterioration in receivables or margin guidance.
