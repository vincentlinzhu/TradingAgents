## Trading plan

### For existing long positions

**Recommendation: HOLD, but tighten risk controls.**

Existing longs can remain in the trade as long as MU stays above the 10-day EMA area near **940–950** or quickly reclaims the **1,036–1,080** zone. The trend remains bullish, but the current reversal means traders should protect profits.

Possible risk-management approaches:

- **Aggressive trailing stop:** below **971.68**, the 2026-06-04 intraday low.
- **Trend-based stop:** below the 10-day EMA zone near **940–950**.
- **Volatility-based stop:** approximately 2 ATRs below the current price, near **880–885**, for traders willing to tolerate wider swings.

The appropriate stop depends on time horizon and position size. Short-term traders should use tighter stops. Longer-term trend followers can use wider volatility-adjusted stops but should reduce position size accordingly.

### For new buyers

**Do not chase here.**

MU remains bullish, but the risk/reward is not attractive for fresh entries after such an extended move and with RSI above 80. Better entry setups would include:

1. **Pullback entry:**  
   Consider buying only if MU pulls back toward **940–970** and shows evidence of stabilization, such as a reversal candle, reduced selling volume, or a reclaim of the 10-day EMA.

2. **Breakout entry:**  
   Consider buying strength only if MU reclaims **1,036**, then **1,080–1,090**, preferably on strong volume and with a controlled intraday range.

3. **Avoid buying if:**  
   MU closes below the 10-day EMA and volume remains elevated. That would suggest the current move is evolving into a correction.

### For short sellers

Shorting MU is risky because the primary trend remains very strong. A tactical short may only be justified if:

- Price fails repeatedly near **1,036–1,080**, and
- MU closes below **941**, and
- MACD histogram begins contracting or MACD crosses below the signal line.

Until then, shorting is countertrend and high-risk.
