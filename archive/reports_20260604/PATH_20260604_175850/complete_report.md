The bull is leaning hard on Q1 revenue of about **$418.4 million**, up roughly **17% YoY**.

That was a good quarter. I’m not disputing that.

But the full-year guide is the reality check.

UiPath guided FY2027 revenue to about **$1.78 billion** versus FY2026 revenue of roughly **$1.61 billion**. That implies only about **10.6% growth**.

That is the core issue.

A true AI software inflection should show up in forward guidance more clearly. Instead, management is effectively guiding to low-double-digit growth. The bull calls that “conservative.” Maybe. But conservative guidance can also mean management sees uneven demand, deal lumpiness, and limited confidence in sustained acceleration.

And Q2 guidance of **$395 million to $400 million** is below Q1 revenue of **$418.4 million**. Again, maybe seasonality plays a role. But if bulls want to claim durable reacceleration, the forward numbers need to support it more convincingly.

Right now, the bull is buying a mid-teens growth story while management is guiding closer to **10%**.

That gap matters.

---

## 2. “AI in 16 of the top 20 deals” is interesting — but still not monetization proof

The bull keeps saying AI is commercially relevant because **16 of the top 20 deals included AI components** and AI-related expansions were reportedly larger.

That is encouraging. But it is not enough.

We still do not know:

- How much revenue came directly from AI products.
- Whether AI was priced separately.
- Whether AI improved net retention.
- Whether AI drove premium pricing.
- Whether AI features have better or worse margins.
- Whether AI adoption repeats next quarter.
- Whether AI changes ARR growth in a measurable way.

This is the key distinction: **AI appearing in deals is not the same as AI changing the financial trajectory.**

In 2026, every enterprise software vendor has “AI components.” Microsoft, ServiceNow, Salesforce, SAP, Oracle, Adobe — all of them are embedding AI. AI attach is becoming table stakes, not necessarily a differentiator.

If UiPath wants a higher multiple, it needs to prove AI is not just helping sales conversations but actually driving sustained ARR acceleration, pricing power, retention, and margin expansion.

So far, management’s full-year guide does not prove that.

---

## 3. Competition is not just a generic risk — it could cap the entire re-rating

The bull argues UiPath has value as a cross-platform automation layer. I agree that this is UiPath’s best strategic argument.

But the competitive threat is still severe.

UiPath is fighting against companies with deeper distribution and stronger bundle economics:

- **Microsoft** can bundle automation through Microsoft 365, Azure, Copilot, and Power Platform.
- **ServiceNow** controls key enterprise workflow layers.
- **Salesforce** can embed automation directly into CRM and service workflows.
- **SAP and Oracle** sit inside ERP and core business processes.
- AI-native startups can attack narrower workflows with lighter, faster tools.

The bull says large enterprises are messy and need cross-platform orchestration. True. But that does not mean UiPath captures attractive economics.

If Microsoft or ServiceNow offers “good enough” automation inside existing contracts, UiPath can face pricing pressure. If AI-native agents reduce reliance on traditional RPA infrastructure, UiPath may need to spend more aggressively just to defend relevance.

That is why the market is skeptical. Not because investors do not understand the AI narrative — but because they understand that AI automation is becoming a crowded, bundled, highly competitive category.

---

## 4. Profitability is improving, but it is still early and not especially high quality

The bull says operating leverage is “undeniable.” Directionally, yes.

Operating income improved from:

- **-$348.3 million** in FY2023
- **-$164.7 million** in FY2024
- **-$162.6 million** in FY2025
- **+$56.8 million** in FY2026

That is progress.

But let’s keep scale in mind. FY2026 operating income of **$56.8 million** on **$1.61 billion** of revenue is only about a **3.5% operating margin**. TTM operating margin is better at **7.27%**, but that is still modest for a software company with **80%+ gross margins**.

And headline net income quality remains a concern. FY2026 net income was **$282.3 million**, but pretax income was only **$100.6 million**, helped by a **negative tax provision of $181.7 million**.

So yes, profitability has improved. But bulls are treating early profitability as if the company has already proven durable software-level margins. It has not.

PATH still needs to prove it can expand margins while also investing enough to compete in AI automation.

## 5. The bull’s valuation argument depends heavily on free cash flow — but SBC changes the picture

This is still my biggest pushback.

The bull highlights:

- TTM free cash flow of about **$522.6 million**
- FY2026 free cash flow of **$352.2 million**
- EV/FCF around **9x**

On the surface, that looks cheap.

But stock-based compensation is very high.

FY2026 SBC was **$290.7 million**, or about **18% of revenue**. FY2026 free cash flow was **$352.2 million**. That means SBC was roughly **83% of FY2026 free cash flow**.

That is not a rounding error. It is a major economic cost.

The bull says subtracting all SBC from FCF is “too punitive.” I disagree. Shareholders absolutely pay for SBC through dilution unless the company spends cash to offset it — and UiPath is doing exactly that.

Buybacks:

- FY2025: **$390.8 million**
- FY2026: **$329.1 million**

FY2026 buybacks consumed almost the entire annual free cash flow.

So investors should be careful giving PATH full credit for “strong FCF” while ignoring that a large portion of that cash is being used to neutralize dilution from employee compensation.

Yes, share count declined from **553.5 million** to **537.0 million**, which is positive. But that decline required substantial cash. It is not free.

The cleaner conclusion is: **PATH is cash-generative, but the owner-earnings profile is much less attractive after accounting for SBC and buybacks.**

## 6. “Cheap” does not automatically mean mispriced

- **12.9x forward earnings**

But valuation has to be connected to quality and durability.

Operating margins are still modest.

SBC is still high.

AI revenue is not disclosed.

Competition is intense.

The stock remains below its 200-day moving average.

So yes, the multiple is discounted. But discounted multiples are appropriate when the market has legitimate concerns about growth durability and competitive positioning.

The bull says the stock only needs “normalization.” I’d say normalization requires proof — and PATH has not provided enough of it yet.

Cheap software stocks can stay cheap for a long time if growth does not accelerate.

- **$10.00–$10.30**: base risk zone

In other words, even the bull’s playbook says the current price around **$11.67** is not an especially clean entry.

If PATH loses **$11.25**, then **$10.70–$10.75**, the recovery thesis weakens quickly. A move back toward **$10.00–$10.30** or even the **$9.20** low area is plausible in a high-volatility stock.

The bull says PATH is down **26%–27% YTD**, and that creates opportunity.

Maybe. But it also reflects damaged confidence.

This stock is down sharply despite AI being one of the strongest equity themes in the market. That tells us investors are not simply missing the story. They are questioning whether UiPath can actually convert AI interest into durable financial acceleration.

The market’s concerns are rational:

- RPA category maturity
- Low-double-digit full-year growth guidance

- Fierce platform competition
- Modest operating margins
- High SBC

- Mixed analyst sentiment

A beaten-down stock can be an opportunity. But it can also be a value trap if the turnaround takes longer than bulls expect.

PATH has not yet shown enough to rule out the value-trap risk.

## Final bear view: improved company, still not a Buy

I am not arguing UiPath is broken. It has real strengths:

- ARR above **$1.9 billion**
- Gross margins above **80%**
- Net cash around **$1.39 billion**
- Positive free cash flow
- Better Q1 revenue growth
- Early AI deal traction

But the bull is moving faster than the evidence.

The biggest risks remain:

- FY2027 guidance implies only **~10.6% growth**
- Q2 guidance is below Q1 revenue
- AI monetization is not quantified
- Competition from Microsoft, ServiceNow, Salesforce, SAP, Oracle, and startups is intense
- Operating margin remains modest
- FY2026 net income was tax-benefit aided
- SBC remains high at **18% of revenue**
- Buybacks consume much of free cash flow
- Stock failed near the **200-day SMA around $13.02**
- PATH remains down **26%–27% YTD**
- BofA still rates the stock **Underperform**

So my conclusion is straightforward:

**Do not chase PATH here.**

For existing holders, this is a defensive Hold with strict attention to **$11.25** and especially **$10.70–$10.75**. For new investors, I would wait for real confirmation: sustained ARR acceleration, clearer AI monetization, lower SBC, durable margin expansion, and a stock price that can hold above **$13.00–$13.20**.

The bull is buying improving probability. I’d rather wait for proof.

Right now, PATH remains a volatile turnaround story — not a high-conviction investment.
