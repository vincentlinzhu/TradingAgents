But “plausible” is not the same as “proven.”

The Cognizant integration with AI Control Tower is strategically interesting. But it does not yet prove material contribution to:

- Subscription revenue growth
- Net expansion
- RPO/cRPO
- Pricing power
- Customer retention
- Margin expansion
- Large-deal conversion

Right now, the AI story is still mostly qualitative.

And AI is not a one-way positive. It could also disrupt the model:

- AI agents may pressure traditional seat-based pricing.
- Hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon can bundle AI workflow and governance tools.
- Salesforce, Workday, Atlassian, and AI-native startups are attacking adjacent workflow categories.
- Customers may delay purchases while evaluating their AI architecture.
- AI development and infrastructure could keep pressuring margins before revenue meaningfully ramps.

So I reject the idea that AI automatically strengthens the bull case. It is an opportunity, yes — but also a source of competitive and margin risk.

At **~8.2x revenue** and **~40x EBITDA**, investors are already paying for a lot of AI success before the financial evidence is visible.

## 8. Macro is not background noise for a stock like NOW

Bull, you say macro is not company-specific impairment. True. But stocks trade on discount rates and risk appetite, not just company fundamentals.

NOW is still a long-duration growth equity. That makes it sensitive to:

- Treasury yields
- Fed rate expectations
- Strong economic data delaying rate cuts
- Nasdaq and software-sector flows
- Enterprise IT spending confidence
- Risk-off headlines from geopolitical tensions

Recent news already showed this vulnerability: NOW and other software names fell after a stronger-than-expected jobs report raised higher-for-longer rate concerns.

That matters. A company can keep growing and still see its stock fall if multiples compress. Business quality does not immunize shareholders from valuation risk.

And at **68x trailing earnings**, the stock still has plenty of room to de-rate if rates stay elevated or SaaS sentiment weakens further.

## 9. The bull case requires more to go right than you admit

You say the bull case only requires ServiceNow to keep doing what it is already doing. I disagree.

At today’s valuation, NOW likely needs several things to go right:

1. Revenue growth remains near or above **20%**.
2. Margins stop compressing.
3. GAAP net income and EPS growth reaccelerate.
4. AI Control Tower becomes measurable revenue.
5. EmployeeWorks becomes material.
6. Deal delays do not worsen.
7. Acquisitions integrate smoothly.
8. SBC becomes less burdensome.
9. Buybacks prove value-accretive.
10. SaaS multiples stabilize.
11. Rates stop pressuring growth stocks.
12. The stock reclaims **$121**, then **$124–$128**, then ultimately **$140–$142**.

That is a long checklist.

The bear case does not require ServiceNow to collapse. It only requires one or two of those assumptions to disappoint. If margins stay pressured, if AI monetization is slower than hoped, if deal delays persist, or if software multiples compress further, NOW can retest lower levels quickly.

## My bottom line

Bull, I respect the quality argument. ServiceNow is a strong enterprise software platform. But the stock still does not offer enough margin of safety here.

The key bear points remain:

- Valuation is still premium.
- Q1 net income grew only **2%** despite **22.1%** revenue growth.
- Gross, operating, and net margins all declined YoY.
- SBC is high at **~14.5% of Q1 revenue**.
- Q1 buybacks exceeded free cash flow.
- AI monetization is promising but not proven.
- EmployeeWorks traction is not yet quantified.
- Deal delays and competition remain real risks.
- Goodwill and intangibles have risen sharply.
- The stock remains below its falling 200-day SMA.
- The recent rally was aggressively rejected.
- Macro remains unfavorable for high-multiple software.

So my stance is clear: **do not chase NOW here.**

For existing holders, this is a **disciplined hold**, not a complacent long. For new investors, I would wait for a better price or stronger confirmation.

Key levels matter:

- **Below $111:** downside risk increases meaningfully.
- **Below $103–$104:** recovery structure weakens.
- **Below $99–$100:** medium-term trend failure.
- **Above $121:** first real technical improvement.
- **Above $124–$128:** stronger confirmation.
- **Above $140–$142:** long-term trend repair.

Until NOW proves margin recovery, quantifies AI monetization, and reclaims key technical levels, I would not call this an attractive accumulation opportunity. I would call it what it is: **a high-quality stock with a still-unfavorable risk/reward profile.**
