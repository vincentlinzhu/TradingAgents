**Investment Thesis**: The final decision is Hold because the analysts broadly agree that DRAM remains a compelling but high-risk expression of AI-driven memory demand, especially HBM, while the current risk/reward is not strong enough to justify new aggressive buying. The bullish case is still valid: AI servers require rising memory intensity, HBM supply remains a key bottleneck, memory pricing can create substantial earnings leverage, and DRAM offers a more targeted way to participate in that theme than broader semiconductor or AI ETFs. DRAM also has not technically broken down: the $55.40–$55.80 support zone has held twice, including lows around $55.38 and $55.45, the June 9 close near $59.86 remained above the approximate Bollinger midpoint near $58, and price is still well above the 50-day SMA around $46.29.

However, the conservative and neutral analysts made the stronger argument on near-term sizing and risk discipline. DRAM ran from roughly $27.76 to nearly $70 in about two months, then fell roughly 20% in two sessions, including a 15% one-day decline. That sequence signals more than ordinary volatility: it shows crowding, fragility, and potential distribution after a parabolic move. The $1 billion-plus inflow near the June 3 peak may indicate sponsorship, but it also raises the risk of trapped holders selling into rebounds. The June 9 session reinforced this concern because DRAM opened near $63.62, failed almost exactly at the $63.60 repair zone, dropped back toward support, and closed below the 10-day EMA around $61.59.

Technicals remain damaged enough to prevent a Buy or Overweight rating. DRAM is below the 10-day EMA, MACD has crossed bearishly with the MACD line around 4.68 below the signal line near 5.05, and ATR near $4.28 means the stop at $55.40 may not execute cleanly if volatility accelerates. RSI cooling from above 80 to the high-50s removes some froth, but it is not an oversold or fully repaired setup. Valuation around 28.6x trailing earnings also leaves limited margin for error in a cyclical memory exposure if memory pricing, AI capex expectations, or supplier guidance disappoint.

The portfolio implementation should therefore be a risk-managed Hold, not a passive Hold. Maintain moderate exposure around 55%–60% of intended long-term allocation, keep dry powder, and do not add simply because support has held. A modest add can be considered only after DRAM closes above $63.60 with better volume and less intraday rejection; a stronger move toward full target exposure should wait for DRAM to reclaim and hold $65.70 with improving momentum. If DRAM rallies into $63.60–$65.70 and fails again, trim oversized positions. If DRAM closes decisively below $55.40, reduce exposure materially, with downside risk then extending toward $52–$51 and potentially the 50-day area near $46.30.

**Time Horizon**: 1-3 months tactical review; longer-term thematic exposure only if support holds and technical repair is confirmed
