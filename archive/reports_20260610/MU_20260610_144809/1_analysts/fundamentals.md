## Executive View

MU’s latest fundamentals show an exceptionally strong cyclical upturn: revenue, margins, EBITDA, net income, operating cash flow, and free cash flow have all expanded sharply. The most recent quarter, ended **2026-02-28**, appears to represent a major profitability inflection, with quarterly revenue of **$23.86B**, gross margin of roughly **74.4%**, operating margin of roughly **67.6%**, and net income of **$13.785B**.

Debt has declined from prior quarters, while equity and tangible book value have risen substantially.

The main concern is valuation and cyclicality. MU’s **trailing P/E of 42.2x** and **price/book of 13.9x** suggest a large amount of future strength is already priced in, while the stock’s **beta of 2.17** indicates high volatility. However, the **forward P/E of roughly 8.0x** and **PEG ratio of 0.32** suggest consensus forward earnings growth is extremely strong relative to the current valuation.

**Actionable stance:** MU remains fundamentally attractive for traders willing to accept semiconductor-cycle volatility. The data supports a **BUY**, preferably on pullbacks or using staggered entries, rather than chasing aggressively after sharp momentum moves.

**Ticker:** MU

## Key Risks for Traders

### 1. Semiconductor Memory Cyclicality

MU operates in a historically cyclical market. Margins can expand rapidly during shortages and pricing upcycles, but they can also contract quickly if supply catches up or end demand slows.

### 2. Valuation Risk

Although the forward P/E of **8.0x** is attractive, the trailing P/E of **42.2x** and price/book of **13.9x** show the market has already rewarded MU significantly. If forward earnings expectations are revised down, the stock could re-rate lower quickly.

### 3. High Volatility

MU’s beta of **2.173** indicates materially higher volatility than the broader market. Position sizing should reflect this.

### 4. Capex Intensity

Capital expenditure remains heavy. The latest quarter’s capex was **$6.387B**. If demand slows, high fixed costs and high investment spending could pressure free cash flow.

### 5. Receivables Growth

Accounts receivable nearly doubled quarter over quarter. Traders should monitor whether cash collections remain strong in future reports.
