MACD is below its signal line, with MACD falling from around 126 to 96.74. RSI has reset sharply from above 82 to 59.56. RSI near 60 is not bearish in isolation, but the speed of the reset confirms a momentum shock. The prior clean uptrend has changed character. That matters.

The distance from trend anchors also matters. MU is roughly 46% above its 50-day SMA and more than 150% above its 200-day SMA. I agree those averages may reflect an older earnings regime, but they still show how little technical cushion exists beneath the stock. If sentiment cools, MU does not need bad fundamentals to fall sharply. It only needs positioning to unwind.

On fundamentals, I acknowledge the numbers are outstanding: revenue of $23.86B, gross margin around 74%, operating margin around 68%, net income of $13.785B, operating cash flow of $11.9B, free cash flow of $5.5B, falling debt, and cash exceeding total debt. Those support holding existing exposure. They do not automatically justify adding meaningful new exposure after a parabolic move.

The forward P/E near 8x is compelling only if forward EPS around $111.51 is durable. In memory semiconductors, forward estimates can change quickly. DRAM pricing, HBM execution, AI server demand, capex response, Samsung and SK Hynix competition, and inventory dynamics can all shift expectations. The trailing P/E above 42x and price/book near 14x are not irrelevant; they tell us expectations are already elevated. A “cheap” forward multiple can look cheap precisely when margins are near peak optimism.

Receivables also should not be dismissed. Accounts receivable rose from roughly $8.0B to $15.4B quarter over quarter. Strong operating cash flow reduces the concern, but it does not eliminate the need for another data point. For a conservative process, that is a monitoring flag, especially when the market is already capitalizing a very optimistic forward earnings path.

The lack of recent MU-specific news is not a bullish catalyst. It means this move is being driven by already-known fundamentals, AI-sector enthusiasm, and broad semiconductor flows. That may be enough for an aggressive trader, but not enough for a conservative desk to increase exposure after a volatility shock. The social/news and macro reports both support HOLD, not aggressive buying. Hot inflation and geopolitical escalation fears can pressure high-beta semiconductors regardless of company-level strength.

So I remain opposed to the original BUY at 40–60% intended exposure. The safest stance is HOLD for existing positions with tighter risk controls. If participation is mandatory, I would only approve a minimal conditional starter, closer to 20–25% of intended exposure, and only near $900–920, not above it.

I would not add between $950 and $990 because that is directly below the $989–1,000 resistance area where MU already failed intraday. I would require either a calmer retest of $900–920 with narrower ranges, continued VWMA support, and a reclaim of the 10-day EMA, or a confirmed close above $990–1,000 with follow-through above $1,035 before increasing exposure.

Risk controls should be layered: a close below $919 VWMA is a caution signal, a close below $900 should trigger reduction or hedging, and a decisive break below $854–864 should be treated as tactical failure. If that break occurs, reassess closer to $790–800 or wait for stabilization.

My final conservative stance is HOLD, or at most a very small conditional starter if the firm insists on exposure. MU may be a long-term winner, but this is not a low-risk entry. The prudent path is to preserve capital, avoid oversized exposure in a high-ATR tape, and demand confirmation before committing meaningful firm assets.
