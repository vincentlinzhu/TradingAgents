## 4. Margin pressure is not a rounding error.

The bull says bears are cherry-picking margins because EBITDA improved. But the GAAP margin deterioration is real.

Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025:

- Gross margin fell from **78.9% to 75.1%**
- Operating margin fell from **14.6% to 13.3%**
- Net margin fell from **14.9% to 12.4%**

That is not trivial. A nearly **380 basis point decline in gross margin** is meaningful for a software business, especially one priced as a high-quality compounder.

Yes, EBITDA grew 30%. But if gross margin is compressing and GAAP profitability is lagging revenue growth, investors should ask whether the business is becoming more costly to scale.

The bull calls R&D and sales spending “growth investment.” Maybe. But investors have heard that argument many times across software. Spending is only value-accretive if it produces future operating leverage. Right now, the evidence is mixed.

ServiceNow is spending heavily:

- R&D: **21.8% of revenue**
- SG&A: **39.9% of revenue**
- Selling and marketing alone: **32.3% of revenue**

For a mature large-cap software company already generating nearly **$14B** in trailing revenue, that is a large expense base. If revenue growth slips even modestly, margin pressure can become a bigger issue quickly.

## 5. Free cash flow is strong, but the quality of that cash flow deserves scrutiny.

The bull’s strongest point is free cash flow. I won’t deny it: NOW generated roughly **$5.11B** in trailing free cash flow and had a trailing FCF margin around **36.6%**. That is impressive.

But the bull treats free cash flow as if it eliminates every other concern. It does not.

First, SaaS free cash flow can be heavily influenced by billing cycles and deferred revenue dynamics. Q1 and Q4 are seasonally strong cash flow quarters. In the recent data:

- Q1 2026 FCF: **$1.529B**
- Q4 2025 FCF: **$2.000B**
- Q3 2025 FCF: **$569M**
- Q2 2025 FCF: **$526M**

This is not a smooth quarterly cash generation pattern. Investors should avoid annualizing the best quarters too aggressively.

Second, ServiceNow’s current liabilities are large, with a current ratio of only **0.845** and working capital of **negative $1.55B**. Yes, deferred revenue explains much of that. But deferred revenue is still an obligation to deliver services. It is not free money.

Third, free cash flow is being used aggressively to offset dilution and fund acquisitions. In Q1:

- Free cash flow: **$1.529B**
- Stock repurchases: **$2.225B**
- Business purchases: **$1.325B**

Repurchases exceeded free cash flow in the quarter. Add acquisition spending, and capital deployment was far above FCF. That may be manageable because the balance sheet is strong, but it undercuts the idea that all free cash flow is freely available to shareholders.

## 6. Stock-based compensation remains a real shareholder cost.

Q1 2026 stock-based compensation was:

- About **14.5% of revenue**

That is a significant cost. The company is buying back stock to offset dilution, but that means a meaningful portion of cash generation is being used defensively rather than creating incremental shareholder value.

Yes, diluted shares declined modestly from **1.045B to 1.040B**. But the company had to spend **$2.225B** in one quarter to help achieve that. If the stock is expensive, buybacks are less accretive. If the stock keeps falling, those repurchases may prove poorly timed.

So I would not call SBC “thesis-breaking,” but I would absolutely call it a valuation headwind.

## 7. The AI alliance is strategically interesting, but not yet financially meaningful.

The bull spends a lot of time on the ServiceNow–NICE AI alliance. I understand why. It fits the narrative: front-office customer engagement flowing into back-office workflow automation.

But investors need to separate **strategic narrative** from **financial proof**.

What do we know from the data provided?

We know the partnership exists. We do not know:

- How much incremental revenue it will generate.
- Whether it improves renewal rates.
- Whether it increases average contract value.
- Whether customers will pay meaningfully more for the AI functionality.

That is the key issue. The market has become more selective about AI stories. “AI positioning” is no longer enough. Investors want measurable monetization.

The bull says ServiceNow owns the workflow layer where AI can drive outcomes. That may be true. But the stock already reflects a lot of that optimism. If AI monetization is slower than expected, the multiple can compress before the long-term story ever proves out.

## 6. Margin pressure is real

You argue margin pressure is just investment. Maybe. But that is the optimistic interpretation.

The reported margins deteriorated meaningfully:

- Gross margin declined from **78.9% to 75.1%**
- Operating margin declined from **14.6% to 13.3%**
- Net margin declined from **14.9% to 12.4%**

A nearly **380 basis point gross margin decline** is significant for a software company. Gross margin is supposed to be one of the most durable advantages of the model.

Yes, EBITDA grew **30%**, and EBITDA margin improved. But EBITDA excludes costs that matter, especially when SBC and acquisition activity are material.

The expense base is also heavy:

- R&D: **21.8% of revenue**
- SG&A: **39.9% of revenue**
- Selling and marketing: **32.3% of revenue**

If revenue growth stays above 20%, maybe the company can absorb this. But if growth slows even modestly, the operating leverage issue becomes much more obvious.

The bull case assumes future margin recovery. I would rather wait to see evidence of it.

## 7. Free cash flow is strong, but not spotless

I agree: free cash flow is the best part of the story.

ServiceNow generated:

- **$5.11B** trailing free cash flow
- **36.6%** trailing FCF margin
- **$1.529B** Q1 2026 FCF

That is impressive. But it does not erase the risks.

Third, capital deployment is aggressive:

- Q1 FCF: **$1.529B**

Buybacks alone exceeded quarterly free cash flow. Add acquisitions, and cash usage was far above FCF. That may be manageable because the balance sheet is strong, but it weakens the argument that the free cash flow is fully available to shareholders.

## 9. AI is still promise, not proof

The ServiceNow–NICE alliance is strategically interesting. I understand the bull case: AI becomes more valuable when it can trigger workflows, and ServiceNow sits in the system-of-action layer.

But investors need to separate strategic logic from financial evidence.

We do not yet know whether the NICE partnership will:

- Generate meaningful incremental revenue
- Increase renewal rates
- Raise average contract values
- Improve pricing power
- Expand margins
- Offset AI infrastructure costs
- Defend against competitive replication

That last point is important. ServiceNow is not alone in enterprise workflow automation. Microsoft, Salesforce, Atlassian, cloud platforms, CRM vendors, ITSM competitors, and AI-native automation vendors all want a piece of this market.

The bull says ServiceNow is best positioned to monetize AI. Maybe. But the stock already reflects a lot of that optimism. If AI monetization is slower than expected, or if AI raises costs before it lifts revenue, the multiple can compress.

“AI optionality” is not enough reason to buy a technically damaged stock at a premium valuation.
