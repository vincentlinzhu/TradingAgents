Our mandate is not merely to make sure losses are survivable; it is to avoid low-probability or poorly timed trades when better confirmation may be available soon.

The neutral analyst’s compromise is more sensible, but even there I would be careful. A tiny pilot position near $103–$105 is acceptable only as an exploratory exception, not as an endorsement of the BUY proposal. If the position must be reduced to one-quarter or one-third of a first tranche, with no additions until $113–$116 and no overweight discussion until $124–$128, then the correct official stance is not BUY. It is HOLD with permission for a very small tactical probe if the portfolio manager insists on participating.

I also reject the aggressive argument that waiting for $113–$116 is simply “giving up” 10%–13%. That is an overly return-focused framing. Waiting for $113–$116 buys evidence that NOW has reclaimed the 10-day EMA and VWMA zone. That is not emotional comfort; it is confirmation that buyers are regaining control. In a damaged, volatile stock, paying for confirmation can be prudent insurance. The first 10% of a rebound is not free money if the probability of a support failure is elevated.

The overhead supply is also being understated. The $113–$116 zone is not just a first upside target; it is resistance. The $119–$128 region contains recent breakdown supply. The $135–$140 area includes the June 1 high and the declining 200-day SMA. There are multiple layers of potential selling pressure above the stock. The aggressive analyst calls that a “roadmap.” From a conservative perspective, those are hurdles that should be cleared before committing new capital.

On RSI, I agree the stock is no longer overbought, but that is not a buy signal. RSI near 50 is neutral. It does not show capitulation, and it does not confirm reversal. A fall from nearly 78 to around 50 confirms that momentum cooled sharply. It does not prove the decline is over.

Fundamentally, I acknowledge ServiceNow is a strong company. Revenue growth of about 22.1%, trailing free cash flow of roughly $5.11 billion, FCF margins in the mid-30% range, net cash, and a large deferred revenue base are all positives. But the aggressive argument leans too heavily on the best metrics while minimizing the weaknesses. NOW still trades around 63x trailing GAAP earnings, roughly 37x EV/EBITDA, and 7.8x trailing sales. That is not distressed pricing. It requires continued execution.

And the most recent fundamental data is not flawless. Q1 revenue grew strongly, but net income increased only about 2.0% and diluted EPS only about 2.3%. Gross margin declined from 78.9% to 75.1%, and operating margin also softened. Stock-based compensation was $547 million, about 14.5% of revenue. Buybacks of $2.225 billion exceeded Q1 free cash flow of $1.529 billion. Yes, the company has balance-sheet flexibility, but buybacks are partly offsetting dilution, not purely creating incremental shareholder value.

The acquisition issue is also real. Goodwill and intangibles increased from $1.54 billion to $6.02 billion year over year. That may eventually prove strategic, but until integration benefits are visible, it adds execution risk, product complexity risk, margin risk, and possible future impairment risk. The aggressive analyst calls this optionality; I would call it unproven capital deployment.

The AI/NICE partnership is encouraging, but it should not override the technical and valuation risks. It is not yet evidence of incremental revenue, higher renewal rates, stronger pricing power, or margin expansion. In a premium-valued software stock, we should demand more than a credible narrative before initiating meaningful exposure. The market may reprice early, but it can also overprice AI enthusiasm and then punish disappointment.

Macro risk is another reason to avoid front-loading. The world affairs report explicitly flags Nasdaq weakness, chip-led selloffs, and inflation data as near-term risks. NOW is exactly the type of high-multiple software stock that can sell off if yields rise or risk appetite weakens. Saying “the market already knows CPI is coming” does not mean the risk is priced in. Known catalysts still cause sharp moves when the outcome surprises.

So my low-risk adjustment remains clear: do not approve the BUY as written. Maintain HOLD for new capital. Existing holders can continue holding while $99–$100 remains intact, but they should reassess quickly if NOW closes below the 50-day SMA near $99.42. A break there opens downside toward $95 and then $89–$91.

For new buying, I would wait for one of two things: either a confirmed support hold with improving volume and price stabilization, or a close above $113–$116 that reclaims the 10-day EMA and VWMA zone. No additions should occur below that level. No overweight discussion should occur until the stock clears at least $124–$128 and broader software/Nasdaq breadth stabilizes.

If management insists on participating now, limit it to a very small pilot position with immaterial portfolio risk. But that should be viewed as a tactical exception, not a BUY program.

ServiceNow is a strong business, but the stock is technically damaged, volatile, valuation-sensitive, and exposed to macro multiple compression. The safest and most sustainable path for the firm’s assets remains HOLD, not BUY.
