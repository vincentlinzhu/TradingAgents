So the Aggressive Analyst’s upside ladder is not simply a set of targets; it is a series of supply zones the stock has not yet proven it can absorb.

Momentum also does not support a fresh BUY. MACD is still positive, but it is deteriorating and below the signal line, with MACD around 1.89 versus signal around 3.83. RSI around 48 is neutral, not constructive. The 10-day EMA has rolled over. Price is below VWMA. The 200-day SMA is declining. The Aggressive Analyst is right that support entries happen before lagging indicators fully turn, but that does not mean every support entry is worth taking. Here, the weight of evidence says the stock is still in repair mode.

On fundamentals, I agree that ServiceNow is a high-quality business. Revenue growth around 22%, TTM free cash flow of $5.11B, strong gross margins, and a net-cash-like balance sheet are real positives. But quality of company is not the same thing as quality of entry. The fundamental report itself concludes HOLD, not BUY, because the margin of safety is not obvious.

The risks are not trivial: TTM P/E around 60.3x, price/book near 8.9x, gross margin compression from roughly 78.9% to 75.1%, stock-based compensation at about 14.5% of revenue, Q1 buybacks of $2.23B exceeding quarterly free cash flow of $1.53B, rising goodwill and intangibles, and negative working capital. These are not reasons to abandon the company, but they are reasons not to increase exposure before the chart confirms recovery.

The forward P/E of 20.2x and PEG of 0.99 are only reassuring if forward expectations are achieved. That is exactly the risk. If AI revenue ramps slower than hoped, if margins fail to expand, if enterprise software budgets tighten, or if investors rotate toward cheaper AI software peers, NOW can decline even while remaining a fundamentally strong company.

I also think the Aggressive Analyst is too willing to treat AI potential as de-risked. The $1.5B 2026 Now Assist AI revenue target is encouraging, and IBM, Wipro, and Cognizant are meaningful partners. But partnerships are still pathways, not realized revenue. The market will eventually demand proof in billings, RPO, attach rates, customer expansion, and margin contribution. Until that proof is clearer, we should not let the AI narrative override valuation and technical discipline.

The Salesforce comparison also deserves more respect. If investors see Salesforce as a cheaper enterprise AI platform with stronger near-term Agentforce momentum and better estimate trends, capital can rotate away from NOW even if ServiceNow’s long-term position is attractive. Relative valuation matters in a crowded AI software trade.

Macro does not justify rushing either. Lower yields helped NOW, but that also confirms rate sensitivity. If yields reverse, high-multiple software can sell off quickly. The broader reports also cite mixed tech breadth, late-session tech weakness, and software-sector underperformance over six months. That is not the backdrop where I want to add overweight exposure to a technically damaged growth stock before confirmation.
