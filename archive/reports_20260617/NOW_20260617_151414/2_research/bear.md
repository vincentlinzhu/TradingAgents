
Bear Analyst: ## Bear Case for ServiceNow (`NOW`): Quality Business, Bad Stock Setup, and Too Much Optimism Priced In

I agree with the bull on one thing: ServiceNow is not a broken company. But that is not the same as saying the stock is a buy.

The bullish argument leans heavily on the idea that “the business is fine, therefore the selloff is an opportunity.” That is too simplistic. The market is not just questioning whether ServiceNow can grow. It is questioning whether the current growth, margin profile, AI monetization evidence, and valuation justify owning the stock while the chart is actively breaking down.

My view remains: **SELL / avoid new long exposure** until `NOW` proves stabilization technically and shows clearer fundamental leverage.

This is not a low-quality company. It is a high-quality company with a deteriorating stock trend, premium expectations, weakening margins, heavy stock-based compensation, aggressive capital allocation, and an AI narrative that still needs proof.

---

# 1. The Bull Says “The Chart Is Damaged, But the Business Is Fine.” That Misses the Point.

The bull is trying to separate the technical picture from the fundamental picture. But in a premium-valued growth stock, the technical breakdown often reflects the market’s reassessment of fundamentals before the headline numbers fully show it.

Let’s be clear about the damage:

- `NOW` closed at **95.48** on 2026-06-17.
- It recently peaked at **135.86** on 2026-06-01.
- That is a decline of roughly **29.7% in about two weeks**.
- The stock is down from around **200.98** one year ago, a decline of roughly **52.5%**.
- Price is below the **10 EMA**, **50 SMA**, **200 SMA**, and **VWMA**.
- The 200 SMA is not flat — it is falling, from **154.74** in April to **137.65** in mid-June.

That is not just “volatility.” That is a failed relief rally inside a larger downtrend.

The bull wants to call this a technical reset. I would call it a warning. If a supposedly premier enterprise AI software name is down over 50% in a year and just gave back nearly 30% in two weeks, investors should ask why the market is selling it so aggressively despite all the AI partnership headlines.

---

# 2. Buying Here Is Catching a Falling Knife, Not “Disciplined Accumulation”

The bull suggests staged accumulation near current levels, then adding above **100–103**, and treating **106–108** as confirmation.

But that is internally inconsistent. If confirmation only arrives above **106–108**, why buy aggressively at **95** while the stock is below every key trend measure?

The technical setup is still bearish:

- Latest 10 EMA: **106.81**
- Latest 50 SMA: **99.39**
- Latest 200 SMA: **137.65**
- Latest VWMA: **116.94**
- Latest close: **95.48**

So the stock is:

- About **10.6% below** the 10 EMA.
- Below the 50 SMA after recently reclaiming it and failing.
- Roughly **30% below** the 200 SMA.
- Far below VWMA, meaning recent volume-weighted buyers are likely underwater.

That last point matters. The rally into early June happened on huge volume — including **68M+ shares** on both May 29 and June 1 — and then failed quickly. That creates overhead supply. Trapped buyers often sell into rebounds, which makes rallies less durable.

Also, RSI is not deeply oversold. The latest RSI reading was **45.71** on June 16, down from **78.32** on June 1. Momentum has weakened, yes, but there is no confirmed capitulation signal. The stock can absolutely keep falling before it becomes technically washed out.

The next downside levels are clear:

- Break below **95** opens the door to **90–91**.
- Below that, **87–89**.
- Then a retest of the **83–85** April low zone.

So the bull’s “accumulate now” strategy risks buying before the stock even tests the next major supports.

---

# 3. The Valuation Is Not as Cheap as the Bull Claims

The bull leans hard on the forward P/E of **19x**, the PEG ratio of **0.98**, and the free cash flow yield of roughly **4.7% to 5.2%**.

But there are problems with that argument.

First, the trailing GAAP multiple is still expensive:

- **TTM P/E: 56.8x**
- **TTM EPS: $1.68**

That is not a cheap stock. It is only cheap if forward earnings estimates prove accurate.

Second, the forward P/E requires a huge earnings step-up. Forward EPS is cited at **$5.03**, versus TTM EPS of **$1.68**. That implies the market is assuming a major earnings acceleration. But the latest quarter did not show that kind of GAAP leverage.

Latest quarter:

- Revenue grew about **22.1% YoY**.
- Operating income grew only about **11.5% YoY**.
- Net income rose from **$460M** to **$469M**, only about **2% YoY**.
- Diluted EPS rose from **$0.44** to **$0.45**.

That is the core bear point: revenue is growing, but earnings are barely moving on a GAAP basis.

So when the bull says “forward P/E is only 19x,” I’d respond: that forward multiple depends on a profitability inflection that has not yet shown up clearly in the GAAP numbers.

---

# 4. Margin Trends Are Moving the Wrong Way

The bull says margin concerns are “manageable.” Maybe. But they are still negative indicators.

Latest quarter:

- Gross margin fell from about **78.9%** to **75.1%** year over year.
- Operating margin fell from about **14.6%** to **13.3%**.
- Revenue grew above 20%, but net income barely improved.

For a SaaS company, gross margin compression is not trivial. ServiceNow is supposed to be a scalable software platform. If revenue is growing strongly but gross margin and operating margin are declining, investors need to ask whether AI infrastructure costs, acquisitions, implementation complexity, and go-to-market spend are eating into the model.

The bull argues that ServiceNow is investing for growth. That is always the defense. But premium software stocks get rewarded when they show both growth and operating leverage. Right now, `NOW` is showing growth, but not compelling GAAP leverage.

That makes the valuation more vulnerable.

---

# 5. Stock-Based Compensation Is a Real Cost, Not a Footnote

The bull acknowledges SBC, but downplays it.

Latest-quarter stock-based compensation was **$547 million**.

That exceeded GAAP net income of **$469 million**.

That is a big deal. If employee compensation paid in stock is larger than the profit attributable to shareholders, then GAAP earnings quality deserves scrutiny.

Yes, SBC is common in software. But “common” does not mean “irrelevant.” Heavy SBC dilutes shareholders unless offset by buybacks. And ServiceNow is using buybacks aggressively.

In Q1 2026:

- Free cash flow: **$1.53 billion**
- Share repurchases: **$2.23 billion**

Buybacks exceeded free cash flow by about **$700 million** in the quarter.

The bull says the balance sheet can handle it. Sure, for now. But that does not make it optimal capital allocation. Buying back stock aggressively while the company trades at a premium valuation and margins are compressing can destroy value if the stock continues falling.

And the stock has continued falling.

---

# 6. The Balance Sheet Is Strong, But It Is Deteriorating Around the Edges

I’m not arguing ServiceNow has a bad balance sheet. It does not. It has:

- Cash and short-term investments of **$5.18 billion**
- Total debt of **$2.43 billion**
- Net cash of roughly **$2.75 billion**

That is a strength.

But the direction of several balance sheet items is less comforting:

- Current ratio is **0.845**.
- Working capital is **negative $1.55 billion**, down from positive **$1.01 billion** a year ago.
- Cash and short-term investments declined from **$6.60 billion** in Q1 2025 to **$5.18 billion** in Q1 2026.
- Tangible book value declined from **$8.60 billion** to **$5.71 billion**.
- Goodwill and intangibles rose sharply to **$6.02 billion**, up from **$1.54 billion** a year ago.

The bull correctly notes that deferred revenue affects working capital. Fair. But the bigger point is capital intensity is rising through acquisitions and buybacks, while tangible book is falling and intangibles are increasing.

That adds integration risk. It also raises the bar for management execution.

---

# 7. AI Partnerships Are Positive Headlines, Not Yet Proven Monetization

The bull spends a lot of time on IBM, Wipro, and Cognizant. Those partnerships are strategically interesting. But bulls are treating them like revenue proof. They are not.

The key question is not whether ServiceNow can announce AI partnerships. The question is whether AI is driving:

- Larger deal sizes
- Higher net retention
- Faster bookings growth
- Pricing power
- Incremental revenue
- Sustainable margin expansion

The research itself says investors still need evidence of AI monetization. Partnership announcements are not enough.

Also, systems integrator partnerships cut both ways. IBM, Wipro, and Cognizant work with many software vendors. They are not exclusive ServiceNow sales arms. If enterprise AI becomes more services-heavy and more customized, systems integrators may capture a large share of the economics while ServiceNow faces higher product and support complexity.

The bull says AI governance is a natural fit. Maybe. But it is also an emerging category where many vendors — including AI-native platforms, hyperscalers, security companies, and enterprise software peers — will compete aggressively.

So yes, the AI narrative is credible. But at this valuation, credibility is not enough. Investors need measurable monetization.

---

# 8. AI-Native Competition Is a Bigger Risk Than Bulls Admit

The bull says large enterprises will not rip out mission-critical workflow systems overnight. That is true. But disruption does not need to happen overnight to hurt the stock.

The risk is not necessarily a sudden replacement of ServiceNow. The risk is slower expansion, pricing pressure, lower attach rates, and more difficult upsells as AI-native tools solve pieces of workflow automation more cheaply or flexibly.

ServiceNow’s historical advantage is being the system of workflow record. But AI-native vendors may increasingly become systems of action — automating work directly across applications.

If that happens, ServiceNow has to defend its position by investing more aggressively in AI capabilities. That can pressure margins. We may already be seeing hints of that through gross margin and operating margin compression.

The bull frames AI as purely additive. The bear case is that AI is both opportunity and threat — and the cost of staying relevant may be higher than bulls expect.

---

The bull dismisses Salesforce as merely a relative value argument.

But relative value matters a lot in institutional portfolios.

If portfolio managers want enterprise AI software exposure, they can choose among:

- Salesforce

That matters. `NOW` does not need to be a bad company to underperform. It only needs to be less attractive than alternatives.

In a market where software has lagged broader indexes and AI leadership remains concentrated in hardware and infrastructure, investors may prefer cheaper software names or more direct AI beneficiaries.

# 10. Macro Is Not a Reliable Bull Catalyst

The bull says lower yields could help ServiceNow. True. But that cuts both ways.

`NOW` is a long-duration growth stock. It is sensitive to rates. Recent strength was helped by falling yields and geopolitical de-escalation headlines. But those are not durable company-specific catalysts.

If the Fed sounds hawkish, if inflation concerns return, if energy/geopolitical risk reaccelerates, or if yields rise, premium software multiples can compress quickly.

That is especially dangerous when the stock is already technically weak.

A stock below its 10 EMA, 50 SMA, 200 SMA, and VWMA does not have much cushion if macro turns against it. The bull is effectively betting on both company execution and a supportive macro tape. That is a lot to ask.

A 5% FCF yield can support the stock if execution remains excellent. It does not protect investors if growth decelerates or estimates come down.

The bull is treating free cash flow as a floor. In a derating software stock, it may only be a cushion.

# 2. “The Business Is Not Broken” Is Not a Buy Thesis.

The bull keeps coming back to the same point: ServiceNow is not broken.

I agree. But that is a straw man. I am not arguing bankruptcy, collapse, or terminal decline.

The bear case is that **the stock still has too much optimism embedded in forward assumptions while current signals are deteriorating.**

The latest quarter shows the problem clearly:

- Revenue grew **22.1% YoY**
- Operating income grew only about **11.5% YoY**
- Net income increased from **$460M** to **$469M**
- Diluted EPS rose from **$0.44** to **$0.45**

So yes, sales are growing. But GAAP earnings are barely moving.

That matters because the bull leans heavily on the forward valuation:

- **Forward P/E:** around **19x**
- **PEG:** **0.98**
- **FCF yield:** roughly **4.7% to 5.2%**

Those numbers look attractive only if the earnings ramp actually arrives. But the trailing reality is still demanding:

- **TTM P/E:** **56.8x**
- **TTM EPS:** **$1.68**
- Forward EPS cited: **$5.03**

That is a large required step-up. The company has not yet shown the GAAP leverage to justify treating that forward multiple as safe.

A stock is not cheap just because the spreadsheet says it will be cheap later.

# 3. Margin Compression Is a Real Negative Signal.

The bull calls margin pressure a watch item. I think that understates the risk.

Latest quarter:

- Gross margin declined from approximately **78.9%** to **75.1%**
- Operating margin declined from approximately **14.6%** to **13.3%**
- Net income grew only about **2% YoY**

For a SaaS platform, this matters. ServiceNow is supposed to be a scalable software model. If revenue grows above 20% but margins decline, investors need to ask what is happening underneath:

- Are AI infrastructure costs rising?
- Are implementation/support costs increasing?
- Are acquisitions diluting margins?
- Is go-to-market spending staying elevated?
- Is AI competition forcing defensive investment?
- Are systems-integrator-led deployments less profitable than bulls assume?

The bull says this is investment for growth. Maybe. But every margin-compressing software company says that.

Premium software stocks need to show **growth plus leverage**. Right now, ServiceNow is showing growth, but not enough leverage.

# 4. Free Cash Flow Is Strong — But It Does Not Cancel SBC and Dilution Risk.

The bull is right that free cash flow is a strength:

- Latest-quarter FCF: **$1.53B**
- Quarterly FCF margin: **40.6%**
- Four-quarter/TTM FCF: roughly **$4.62B to $5.11B**

That is good. But it is not a free pass.

The quality-of-earnings issue is significant:

- Stock-based compensation: **$547M**
- GAAP net income: **$469M**

SBC exceeded GAAP net income. That is not a footnote. That is a real economic cost to shareholders.

And management is using aggressive buybacks to offset dilution:

- Q1 free cash flow: **$1.53B**
- Q1 share repurchases: **$2.23B**

Buybacks exceeded free cash flow by about **$700M** in the quarter.

The bull calls that confidence. I call it aggressive capital allocation while the stock is in a steep downtrend, margins are compressing, and forward earnings assumptions are doing most of the valuation work.

Buybacks only create value if the shares are truly undervalued. If estimates come down, those repurchases may simply mask dilution while consuming cash.

# 5. The Balance Sheet Is Good, But the Trend Is Not Clean.

ServiceNow has a net-cash position, and that is a positive:

- Cash and short-term investments: **$5.18B**
- Total debt: **$2.43B**
- Net cash: roughly **$2.75B**

But the bull is too quick to dismiss the balance sheet deterioration around the edges:

- Cash and short-term investments declined from **$6.60B** to **$5.18B** YoY
- Working capital moved from positive **$1.01B** to negative **$1.55B**
- Current ratio is **0.845**
- Tangible book value declined from **$8.60B** to **$5.71B**
- Goodwill and intangibles rose to **$6.02B**, up from **$1.54B**

Yes, deferred revenue affects working capital. But the broader trend is still important: more acquisitions, more goodwill, lower tangible book, and buybacks exceeding quarterly FCF.

That raises execution risk.

# 6. AI Partnerships Are Not Yet AI Monetization.

The bull leans heavily on IBM, Wipro, and Cognizant. I agree those partnerships are strategically positive.

But they are not yet proof of:

- Higher bookings
- Larger deal sizes
- Better net retention
- Pricing power
- Margin expansion
- Incremental AI revenue
- Successful customer deployments at scale

That distinction matters.

Partnerships are leading indicators, but not all leading indicators convert. Systems integrators like IBM, Wipro, and Cognizant partner with many vendors. They are not exclusive ServiceNow sales channels.

There is also a risk that enterprise AI becomes more services-heavy and customized, with integrators capturing much of the economics while ServiceNow absorbs more product complexity, support cost, and R&D burden.

The bull says investors should buy before proof arrives. That is exactly the risk: buying the narrative before it shows up in the financials.

# 7. AI-Native Competition Does Not Need to Destroy ServiceNow to Hurt the Stock.

The bull says enterprises will not rip out ServiceNow overnight. I agree.

But that is not the real risk.

The real risk is subtler:

- Slower expansion inside existing accounts
- Lower attach rates for new modules
- Pricing pressure
- Workflows automated outside the ServiceNow platform
- Higher R&D spend to defend the moat
- Lower incremental margins on AI-related revenue

A sticky installed base protects the core. It does not guarantee high-margin incremental growth.

For a stock relying on a major forward EPS ramp, even modest pressure on growth or margins can trigger another derating.
