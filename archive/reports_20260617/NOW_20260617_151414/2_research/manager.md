**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull made a credible long-term case that NOW is still a high-quality enterprise software platform: revenue is growing about 22% year over year at nearly $14B of TTM scale, free cash flow is very strong, the balance sheet is net cash, deferred revenue provides visibility, and the IBM/Wipro/Cognizant partnerships support the view that ServiceNow can be relevant in AI workflows and governance. I do not think the business is broken. But the bear carried the debate on timing and risk/reward. NOW is below the 10 EMA, 50 SMA, 200 SMA, and VWMA; the high-volume rebound failed; the stock is down sharply from 135.86 to 95.48; and there is no confirmed bottom yet. On fundamentals, the concerns are also real: GAAP earnings leverage is weak versus revenue growth, gross and operating margins compressed, SBC exceeded GAAP net income, buybacks exceeded quarterly FCF, and AI monetization is still more strategic promise than proven financial contribution. That combination makes a full Sell too harsh for a durable compounder, but it also makes Buy/Overweight premature. The stronger conclusion is to reduce exposure and wait for proof that the stock and earnings trajectory are stabilizing.

**Strategic Actions**: For NOW, trim exposure to a below-benchmark/smaller-than-normal position rather than exiting completely. If the trader currently holds a full position, reduce by roughly one-third to one-half and keep only a core stake for long-term optionality. Do not add while NOW remains below 100–103. If NOW breaks below 95, tighten risk controls and consider trimming further, with downside zones at 90–91, 87–89, and 83–85. Rebuild exposure only in stages: first consider moving back toward neutral if NOW reclaims 100–103 with improving volume; add more only on a stronger reclaim of 106–108; and consider returning to Overweight only if NOW recovers the 116–117 VWMA area and fundamentals show margin stabilization, lower SBC intensity, and clearer AI monetization in bookings/RPO/retention or deal size metrics.
