The bull’s best argument is that SpaceX’s IPO validates the commercial space economy. I agree with that. Space is becoming more visible, more liquid, and more institutionally relevant.

But that does not automatically mean `UFO` benefits.

In fact, the actual market reaction showed the opposite: **space proxy trades unwound after SpaceX became directly tradable.** Reports specifically noted that space stocks dropped even as SpaceX initially surged. That is not a small detail. It suggests a meaningful portion of the prior `UFO` demand was not durable, long-term thematic capital — it was substitute exposure.

Before SpaceX was public, investors had limited ways to express the space trade. Now they have a direct vehicle. That reduces the scarcity value of `UFO`.

The bull says the second phase could be “own the ecosystem.” Maybe eventually. But right now the evidence says the first phase is still “sell the proxy.”

And you do not buy into an unwind just because you hope the next phase will be better.

## 2. The technical damage is real, not just “noise”

The bull calls the decline a reset. I call it technical damage.

`UFO` fell from **$67.81 on May 28** to **$50.27 on June 18**, a drop of roughly **25.9%** in less than a month.

That is not a garden-variety pullback. It is a persistent stair-step decline:

- May 28: **$67.81**
- June 1: **$61.08**
- June 5: **$54.71**
- June 12: **$52.92**
- June 18: **$50.27**

That sequence matters. It shows sellers have remained in control across multiple sessions and price zones.

And `UFO` is now below all the key short- and medium-term reference points:

- 10-day EMA: about **$54.05**
- 50-day SMA: about **$55.15**
- VWMA: about **$56.95**
- Current close: **$50.27**

So the ETF is not just slightly weak. It is trading below short-term trend, medium-term trend, and volume-weighted positioning.

The bull says these are “lagging indicators.” Fine — but they are also the market’s clearest evidence that sponsorship has deteriorated. Until `UFO` reclaims **$54–$55**, any bounce should be treated as a relief rally into resistance, not a confirmed reversal.

## 8. The ETF structure is not an automatic advantage

`UFO` risks becoming a middle-ground product:

- Not enough SpaceX exposure to capture the dominant space winner
- Not enough AI/semiconductor exposure to capture the current market leadership
- Too much exposure to volatile second-order space names
- Still vulnerable to broad thematic sentiment swings

That is the competitive weakness here.

Before SpaceX was public, `UFO` had stronger proxy value. After SpaceX’s IPO, that proxy value is reduced. The ETF has to stand on its own fundamentals and holdings quality — and right now, the market is not rewarding it.
